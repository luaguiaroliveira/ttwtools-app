<Parental Incentive and Performance of 9th Grade Students in the Portuguese Language Evaluation at SAEB 2015: Does parental incentive compensate for mean differences by sex?>

<Abstract:>

Since the 1960s, the sociology of education has as one of its central thematic inequalities (FORQUIN, 1995; NOGUEIRA, 1990). From the 1980s, studies on school effectiveness analyze a set of characteristics of schools that are associated with students learning. At the same time, emerges another field of study that analyzes the relationships between family and school pointing out differences for the type of family incentive to schooling (LAHIRE, 1997; THIN, 2006; NOGUEIRA, 2002, 2012). However, there is still a gap in national production relating student achievement in standardized national evaluations to an indicator that brings together parenting practices to monitor children’s schooling. Thus, the objective in this essay is to create a parental incentive indicator based on the data from SAEB 2015, applied to the 9th grade, and to associate it with the performance of municipal and state school students at SAEB 2015 Portuguese Language Evaluation. The results states that the presence of the high parental incentive increases the students' average results, however, the presence of the high parental incentive does not compensate for the difference of means when compared with the student's gender variable. In other words, regardless of the degree of parental incentive received, within the same NSE group, girls perform better in the Portuguese language assessment of SAEB 2015 than boys.

<1-	Introduction.>
 The lack of equity in Basic Education is still one of the major problems of Brazilian Education. Evaluation methodology can be diversify considering their assessment methodology, the scale of analysis and the factors that are considered in a student's assessment. However, evaluation is an important part of the teaching-learning process. Through evaluation, it is possible to identify gaps regarding content that needs to be reviewed in a class, for example. National scale evaluations allow the identification of inequalities in a country with a large territorial dimension such as Brazil. However, how governments deal with the results obtained through these evaluations and how these evaluations are constructed are debatable elements. Carnoy (2009) analyzes the construction process of national evaluations in Cuba, Brazil, and Chile, pointing out differences in the structure of national evaluations and in how the governments of these different countries deal with the results of these evaluations. In this text, is taken the database of questionnaires applied to students from a non-compulsory National Assessment (SAEB) with the intention of identifying patterns of inequality in relation to student groups. This analysis has the intuit of contributing to the mapping of inequalities, hoping that these studies will be used by the authorities to implement changes in the educational system that take into account the differences between groups of students. Thus promoting greater equity in the National Education System.
 There is a wide field of national productions on schooling strategies mobilized by families, including parental incentive practices for schooling. These practices materialize from encouraging reading or performing homework (CARVALHO, 2004; RESENDE, 2013), to the mobilization of more refined family practices associated with the schooling of children, such as the frequency of visits to museums and cultural spaces (BRANDaO, 2012). Coleman (2000) proposes the concept of social capital as an element that adds to schooling. Social capital would be characterized by relationships developed within the family that add positive elements to the process of schooling of children. Alves and Xavier (2017) propose the construction of an indicator of “parental involvement” inspired by Coleman's concept of social capital. Although the authors use this indicator as one of the explanatory variables for the phenomenon of intra-school exclusion, we consider the authors' work to be a fundamental contribution to the field of study that relates student performance and parental incentive. Thus, contributing to the discussion also undertaken in this text.
 Parallel to the studies that show the contributions of parental incentive to schooling, recent research states to an association between characteristics of the public served by the school and their performance. Franco (2007) shows data from the 2004 OECD Report that points out the strong correlation between socioeconomic status (NSE) and school performance in Brazil. In this line of studies, research has shown that there is a strong correlation between NSE and performance. As well as there is a strong correlation between the student’s gender and race with performance in standardized national evaluations. Considering SAEB 2015 data, INEP released the percentage of proficiency in Portuguese Language evaluation according to the student's gender (graph 1). This result points to a higher concentration of girls with proficient performance in the Portuguese Language Evaluation of SAEB than boys. However, this data does not yet present secondary differences between these two groups. Differences that are also strongly correlated with performance, such as family socioeconomic status. Considering this element, the analyzes that will be undertaken here segment the groups by the latent NSE variable that followed distribution criteria that will be specified in the methodological item of this text.
   In research in the CAPES  periods using the word combination “parental participation + performance”, were obtained only two studies that relate directly to this thematic and which use secondary data from the SAEB questionnaire for construction of them analyzes (GONcALVES; FRANCE, 2008; ALVES; XAVIER, 2017). Considering the low frequency of national production on the thematic, it is justified to conduct this study. The objective is to construct an indicator of parental incentive using the answers of the questionnaires of SAEB 2015, applied to students in the 9th grade of elementary public schools   . Then, to compare this indicator with students’ results at SAEB 2015 Portuguese Language Evaluation divided by groups considering the variables of familiar social-economic level and the student’s gender.  The purpose of the research question is to investigate whether parenting practices that encourage student schooling can compensate for differences in averages of the student's gender variable. Thus, this essay can expand national contributions to the field of study on intra-school inequalities, because it investigates the correlation between students’ characteristics and performance in standardized national evaluations.
This text is divided into four parts: brief introduction about the thematic, research conduction methodology, results obtained and possible conclusions from these results. The following item presents considerations on the methodology applied.
<2-	Methodology.>
The methodology is divided into two moments: the first is the description of the parental incentive indicator, considering the data for the whole country, and the association of the indicator to the performance of public (municipal and state) students in the Portuguese language assessment of the SAEB, controlled by the student's socioeconomic level of belonging. The second moment analyzes the intragroup differences, describing the association between the results in the Portuguese Language Evaluation and the degree of parental incentive within the same group of socioeconomic level, controlled by the sex declared by the student. 
Considering the national data, a selection filter was applied to the database of the SAEB 2015 questionnaires database applied to the 9th grade, to select only students from state and municipal education networks. This option was assumed to the extent that, within public education supply networks, there is a greater approximation between characteristics of the public served by state and municipal networks compared to the public served by the federal education network.
The variables related to parental incentive practices were grouped in the “parental incentive” indicator which was divided into groups according to the students' socioeconomic level (NSE): low NSE, moderate NSE, and high NSE.
First of all, it should be noted that the NSE, here, is taken as a theoretical construct for stratification of student groups by a measure of social position that aggregates schooling data of those consumers pattern. That is, the NSE is taken as a latent variable (not directly observed) whose measurement is made by the aggregation of information (education of the responsible and family consumers patterns) from the SAEB 2015 questionnaires applied to the 9th-grade students. However, this data collection instrument has limitations. One is the absence of household income data. Another limitation is a degree of inaccuracy in the responses of the large-scale evaluation questionnaires already identified by other studies (Soares and Andrade, 2006). Tables 2 and 3 present the synthesis of the variables used to construct the Parental Incentive indicator, as well as the variables used to construct the latent NSE variable.
Next, some discussions are explored from the results of the analyses undertaken.
<3-	Results.>
Considering the national data for public schools (municipal and state schools), the hypothesis test of equality of means indicates that, for a reliability level of 95%, the students' Portuguese language proficiency means differ considering the degree of incentive parental received. In the graphs, are taken as reference line the Soares proficiency scale (2006; 2009) that divides proficient and non-proficient students. In the case of the SAEB Portuguese Language Evaluation applied to the 9th grade, the reference score considers the results above 275 points to be proficient. It is observed that, for the average results of public schools (municipal and state) throughout the country, more than 75% of students in the low NSE and low parental incentive group do not have proficiency in Portuguese. The percentage of concentration of Portuguese language proficient students increases according to the NSE group belonging and the degree of parental incentive received, with a higher student concentration of Portuguese language proficient in the group of high NSE and high parental incentive.
For the intragroup mean differences, it is observed that among boys and girls with low NSE and low parental incentive there is a mean difference of 5.7 points in favor of girls. Between boys and girls with low NSE and high parental incentives, this difference is 12.74 points in favor of girls. Considering the moderate NSE group, the mean difference between boys and girls who received low and high parental incentives is 12.74 points and 14.70 points in favor of girls, respectively. Finally, for the high NSE group, the mean difference between boys and girls who received low and high parental incentives is 12.74 points and 14.70 points in favor of girls.
Above, some possible conclusions considering the analyses undertaken. 
<4-	Conclusion.>
The data seem to indicate that the degree of parental incentive increases the students' results in the SAEB 2015 Portuguese Language Evaluation. However, the presence of high parental incentives is not able to compensate for the difference of averages according to the sex declared by the student.
