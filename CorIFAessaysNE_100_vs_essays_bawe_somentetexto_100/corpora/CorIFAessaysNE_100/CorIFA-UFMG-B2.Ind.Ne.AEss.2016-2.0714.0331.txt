<Do the quotas in Brazil’s federal university deny the principle of merit that brings excellence to these universities?>

Recently the Brazil’s federal universities have to reserve half of their positions for students coming from public schools, low-income families and who are of African or indigenous descent. This quota’s politic is an attempt to give underprivileged Brazilians better chances of getting free higher education and thus access to better jobs and consequently to reduce social inequality in Brazil. However, the quotas system has been subject of several critical based on a meritocratic view of the higher education system. One of the longest standing arguments against the quotas is that the quotas in Brazil’s federal university deny the principle of merit that brings excellence to these universities. Nonetheless, it does not make sense to consider only the academic merit as evaluation criteria at a country with greatly social inequality, as Brazil.

Federal universities in Brazil are considered high quality education and also are free of costs. Before the establishment of quotas, the major of spots in the Brazilian public universities were took on by white people of middle class and upper middle class. In general, these families send their children to best private schools in order to ensure admission of their children at the great federal universities. In the other words, the quality higher education was not accessible for poor people who mostly are black people or mestizo people and who had not good economic positions for studying and to achieve high test scores on the "vestibular,". By the way, there is no merit for a candidate who had the privilege of being born in a family with good financial conditions and could study in the best private schools to rank in more advantageous position than candidates who had no choice and studied in public schools of poor quality.

It is important to note that at a society which there are racism and so social inequality part of the privileged social position of white people is result of uneven chance that they have been over the decades in comparison to the poor population, and not simply due to their merit themselves. Even so, evaluate the students considering only academic merit is inappropriate process and leads to a way of discrimination and injustice social.

In fact, the merit brings excellence to these universities, although the merit does not categorize the students according to their intelligence. Therefore, the excellence of Braziliam federal universities can not be associated entirely with the merit of students from higher-income backgrounds. For instance, several studies show that in the universities where the quotas have followed through there were not any education quality depletion. The universities that adopted quotas system initially, such as Federal University of Bahia and Rio de Janeiro State Univesrity, demonstrated that performance both students (“cotistas and non cotistas”) was the same. In addition, these research pointed out that the motivation and encouraging are even more significant for a good fulfillment.

For all that, the quota’s system does not deny the principle of merit and neither decrease education quality of federal universities. The law of quotas has just been restoring new criteria of evaluation in the Brazil’s federal universities so that it can promote a more diverse and democratic higher education.
