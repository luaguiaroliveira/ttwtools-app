<Why FRBR can be a solution to re-link users and libraries?>

By using a library catalog, we have all been frustrated because we needed to go through multiple pages with records of different resources and had no clarity of which ones would most accurately answer our search query. Catalogs are usually compilations of repeated, overly detailed and poorly articulated records, arranged in an unfriendly interface and lacking relationships that facilitate the choice of users by the resources sought or other related resources. However, the experience can be completely different. The development of catalogs according to the guidelines defined by the Functional Requirements for Bibliographic Records (RFBR) can facilitate the search of users and make the navigation experience richer, since the records are presented not only as a listing but based on greater standardization and focused on the conceptual relationships that the different resources establish among themselves.

According to FRBR, the catalog is not a sequence of bibliographic records and a copy of the traditional catalog, but rather the network of interconnected data, enabling the user to perform seamlessly all the necessary functions (IFLA, 2008). In order to allow related data to be displayed in an articulated way as search results, this conceptual model proposes the connection between eleven entities that aim to cover the description of formal, intellectual, authorial and content aspects of documents. This structure is based on the Entity-Relationship model and is more appropriate to the digital environment, as it abandons the logic of composition and presentation inherited from card catalogs and proposes solutions that consider the web browsing experience. Thus, FRBR proposes a definitive entry of catalogs in the digital world and the abandonment of surpassed practices that make users consider them ineffective search engines.

As the resources will be articulated by the eleven entities that structure the model, they can be shown in a more user-friendly way. If the search is made by Macunaíma, from Mário de Andrade, the user will be referred to the novel, but it will also be shown that in that catalog there is a derivative work, which consists of an adaptation of the novel Macunaíma for the theater. The user's first choice is then to address the set of documents related to the original work, or to the set of documents related to the derivative work. With regard to the original work, for example: the novel itself, the translations for other languages, audiobooks, the different editions published and the identification of the place of custody of each one of the items. Unlike the traditional catalog, Le Boeuf argues (2007) that in a catalog structured on the FRBR model the records are hierarchically arranged so it is possible to reflect the level of relationship that they establish with the work sought. In this way, the user has the ability to choose more clearly which documents are best suited to the search query, since it is not necessary to go through an infinite listing of isolated and frequently repeated records. There is comprehensibility about the resources available since different resources can be grouped around the same work, while identical resources are referenced only once and without redundancy.

Failure in representing a portion of the bibliographic universe larger than a record relating to a single item in an orderly way, stands out as one of the most evident deficiencies of the traditional catalogs. The use of FRBR is a path to grant a logical and well-organized arrangement of data relating to surrogates for documents. This conceptual model can orientate online library catalogs to respond better to the needs of users, increasing their ability to discover and choose easily the documents they want. It can also broaden and clarify the existing links between different works, which allows to enhance the effectiveness of the searches made by users and, consequently, their level of satisfaction in the use of these devices.
