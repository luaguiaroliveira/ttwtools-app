The way we used to approach art works in the field of conservation-restoration in Brazil since the begging of the XX century has changed a lot in the last century. Moreover, the reason for it is not just as result of the technological developments but also for an important revolution in the way artists producing art. Different of the Classic – or so-called Academic – Art, the oeuvres produced since 1920 by the avant-garde artists are not focus on the beauty or for a religious function. Along that, with the continuous modification since them, in this days the Contemporary Art could be classified as the art of experience, an type of art that relate to the way we live and our reflections as humans. Since these changes reflect not just in the topic of the interest of the producers but also modify the media and the materials used, the way we, conservators, looked and treat them should suffer modifications as well. 
An artwork from the Classical past, in general are the oeuvres made from the 15th to the 19th centuries, could simplify be classified as religious devotion, mythological, nobilities’ portraits and later landscapes. In all of these classifications of art, at which time, one thing is common: for the expression be considered a real work of art, the artist should be able to mimic the reality and represent it as the way we see it. The most important aspect was the ability and uniqueness of each oeuvre, the way the artist worked: the identity passed by the hand of the artist. The concept of beauty and pleasant art, at these times, were linked to symmetry and how skillful the artist was. Because of these, and others considerations, when one of these artworks needed a intervention of conservation-restoration some guidelines are: the minimum intervention, in order to maintain the artwork close to the original as possible; the use of reversible material and paintings’ technics that are different from the original technics, in order to differentiate from the original and do not pass as made by the artist; and the respect of the materials’ aging, in order to show that artwork are from a different time, and the time has passed and leaved it imprinted this passage in the oeuvre.
From the 20th century, the ways art was represented changed a lot. The technics at the beginning of the movement generically called Modernism were the same as used in the Classical past. Nevertheless, the themes that were object of representation changed a lot. The continuous disfiguration were one of them, changed the mimic shapes to abstracts and geometric forms, also some artists still working with mimicking nature and human forms but they were looking for the representation of figures that are ordinary places and people. In this section of time, that were from 1920 to around 1970, the conservation-restoration of these artworks still generally the same of the Classical ones, and the reason for this is because even the conception of what were represented changed, the materials used to do this, in most of the cases, were the same as the past. Since 1970, another conception of art begun and changed a lot both fields of art and conservation-restoration. 
The Contemporary Art started working with subjects that are not related just to the representation, but related to the experiences provides by the artwork. Most of these are related to how we live, our experiences and feelings as humans. For this be passed by the artists they used a lot of different materials and media to achieved the connection between the main idea and the viewer. This artists’ idea can come from a personal or universal experience and perspective of the facts from the past to the present. At this times the art work, in some cases, are not made direct by the hand of the artist: they project the oeuvre and delegate to others the construction of some parts or of the entire piece. Since the Modernism, beauty was not a goal to achieved, and in Contemporary art, this aspect remains. Another important point related to the material used by contemporary artists is that it are so variable and can be not just an artistic material but a variety as industrial materials, organic material as food, for example, every day products and other hundreds of possibilities dependable of what the artwork needs to take form of the artists’ discourse. Is clear that these changes are significant and interfere how conservators-restorations deal with the problems that can affect the contemporary artworks. The way we used to do is not embracing this works because the propositions differ so much of the Classical Art and it is a time that this topic is gain more and more discussion.
In conclusion, under all the facts presented in this paper, is essential rethink the way conservation-restoration treat contemporary art works. These days is more important, in my point of view, trace new strategies such as documentation, display, and intervention for these works. This focus should be doing now because the artists that made the artworks still alive and can contribute with theirs points of views and considerations, the materials’ research are more developed when compared with the Classical at the time they are made, just for point some reasons. We should continuous research and developing this field with collaboration of different professionals to made these new approaches more universal and coherent with the artistic and material contemporary artworks’ postulates.
