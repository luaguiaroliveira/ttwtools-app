<Taking decision between many and small or few and big hydropower plant.>

<INTRODUCTION>
Brazil has a big territory and watersheds with a great amount of water, what gives us a natural vocation in producing power by hydroelectric. Along the century XXth, big hydropower plants were constructed as a political decision to move the industries and grow the cities, which demanded big investments and resulted in big social and environmental impacts inherent to big hydropowers. During the last years, an alternative based on small power plant (SHP), operating as run-of-river, have been emerged. In this essay it will be discussed the advantages and disadvantages of building few and big or many and small hydropower plants. Particularities of each basin and each construction might be taking account.

<BODY:>
Big hydropower has been considered in the world a clean way to produce power, because it does not produces much greenhouse gas, as coal plants do, or needs nuclear processes, what generates garbage with no solutions. Stimulated by its natural resources and recent environmental issues, Brazil has constructed many hydropower plants, many of them among the largest of the world. Good examples of that are the hydroelectric powerplant Itaipu Binacional, Altamira, Paulo Afonso and Belo Monte. However, big hydroelectric usually result in problems related to high costs, long time construction and various social and environmental impacts.

Trying work around these issues, SHP have been massively stimulated since the beginning of the XXIth century, because they usually do not cause so many problems, although they do not produce the same quantity of power. Therefore, it is necessary to build dozens times more SHP. As an example, in 2017 there are approximately the triple of hydropower in operation related to 2002, and they are spread all over the country. These constructions can influence fishes’ reproduction, the transport of nutrient and sediments and modify the natural cycle of river runoff. This might follows other unknown environmental impacts, with cumulative and synergic bad effects, which must be better studied. All of these impacts are easily observed in the big hydrpowerplants.

<CONCLUSIONS:>
Studies have been realized around the world to investigate the characteristics of each powerplant. One and another way of production has its points. Some basins are extremely sensitive to environmental changes, for example: the Pantanal watershed in water runoff cycles of long period, nutrient and sediment transport because of its lateral and longitudinal runoff; and Amazon watershed because of population that lives in function of annual inundations. One and another way of production has its points, and some aspects can be minimized with techniques in each powerplant that could make one more feasible than other. Considering the peculiarities of plants, more important than define which powerplant model is better in a generic view is to know the features and requirements of each basin and details in the powerplants’ operation to meet the needs. More studies have to be done in order to understand the basins and the generation of power therein.
