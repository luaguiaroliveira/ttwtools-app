<Minas Gerais' Economy at 19th Century and Different Visions About Brazil's History>
Against the common sense which says that the history is based in facts, or in other words, based in na unique interpretation of the facts, the historiography and the historians write about differents interpretations, each one exposing an own vision, even on the same topic. Besides, one of the most studied locations by the Brazilian historiography is the territory of the actual state of Minas Gerais. Especially at the 19th century, the province of Minas Gerais is paramount to understand the Brazil’s domestic plan.
The importance of the domestic plan, or domestic economy and market itself represents an issue of debate. For example, Caio Prado Jr. asserts that the structural fundament of the country since its colonization is the character of an international exporter economy based in agrarian products, the famous “plantation”. As we can see on this piece: “Se vamos à essência de nossa formação veremos que na realidade nos constituímos para fornecer açúcar, tabaco, alguns outros gêneros (...) e em seguida café, para o comércio europeu (...). É com tal objetivo, objetivo exterior, voltado para fora do país e sem atenção a considerações que não fossem de interesse daquele comércio, que se organizaram a sociedade e a economia brasileiras.” (Prado Jr., 1977:31-32). Prado actully is so influent that his ideas founded an “school of thinking” named “Sentido da Colonizacao”. On the other hand, we have historians, such as João Fragoso and Manolo Florentino who claims that more than an international exporter economy, Brazil was a society established on an escravist mode of production, which generates the appearance of domestic circuits of accumulation, beyond the exclusively production for exportation. As said, the great example is Minas Gerais.
The Minas’ gold cycle at 18th century provide the necessary conditions to expand the domestic market: great population increase, great richness and, because of these, great money and services circulation and that circulation is related to diversified activies. The gold economy was the first experience of an integrated market in Brazil. Subsequently, Minas was the province that most imported slaves, but in contradiction with the “plantation” economy, the majority of farmers had no one or just a few slaves, fact that indicates the familiar character of the agriculture, related to the supply of food inside the province. And if we connect the great importation of slaves to the familiar character of agriculture, we can assure that the domestic economy produce richness, sometimes even more than the agrarian exportation activities.
Another argument to support the internal market character of Minas Gerais is its exportation products. 61,6% of the value represents bovines and pigs and 7,2% to cotton material. These products were oriented to other provinces, such as Sao Paulo, Rio de Janeiro and the ones at Northeast. The international exportation represented only 29,8% of the total value, represented by coffee and tobacco. The biggest cities at 18th century, such as Mariana, Ouro Preto, Diamantina, São João del Rey and Barbacena were, at 19th century, clarely also the biggest urban centres, but now oriented to local production activities and especially the commerce, activity which helped a lot of people to become rich and powerful at this time.
Connecting all of these information, we would be not surprised with the 227% growth of population during 1819 and 1872, the biggest at that time. The development of Minas Gerais is important to understand the development of Brazil and how it is oriented nowadays, to not mention the importance to understand the diversity of Brazil’s historiography and its debates, fact that construct and enrich the notions and visions we all have about Minas and our country.
