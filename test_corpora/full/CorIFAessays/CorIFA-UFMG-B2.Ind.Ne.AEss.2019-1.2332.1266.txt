<MURALS IN URBAN ENVIRONMENT>

According to Oxford Dictionary, art is <direct citation deleted>. In the beginning, young people used spray just to sign their names in public spaces but it soon developed to reflect the artists desire for a better society and demand for social justice. This is Graffiti. In this essay, the importance of this artistic expression will be highlighted to avoid misjudging Graffiti as vandalism and in order to assure its relevance to the urban environment.

Derivative from the Italian word graffiato, “scratched”, Graffiti started focusing just on the artist’s sign. It began at the New York neighborhoods at the late 60’s and early 70’s by a group of young people leaving their marks – or tags – on city walls, with aerosol sprays. One of the tags that became notorious at that time was Dimitrios’ TAKI 183 (Figure 1). Years later, Dimitrios would reveal in interviews that he did it in his teenage years just for letting his sign on the metropolis and having some fun while doing it. Thus, it is possible to infer that Graffiti most basic goal was allowing artists to leave their mark on the urban environment.

Since then, Graffiti has evolved to a more consistent form of art. Instead of just tagging, the artists have adopted new methods, such as using stencil and cardboards, to improve results. Also, they have started painting more elaborated and meaningful drawings to ornate blank public walls, often carrying social demands and asking for more humanity. One example is Banksy’s Girl with Balloon (2002), a famous mural in London that portraits a young girl with her stretched arm towards a heart-shaped balloon carried by the wind (Figure 2). Due to those characteristics, Graffiti definitely can be considered an important contribution that artists give to their cities.

However, some authorities still believe Graffiti should be considered vandalism instead. In January of 2017, a Graffiti mural located in Sao Paulo was painted gray by order of the mayor in charge, Mr. Doria. The act revolted Sao Paulo citizens, as the mural was called “the bigger outdoor Graffiti mural in Latin America” by the former mayor, Mr. Haddad, and it was decorated by more than two hundred artists. Fortunately, the act has been judged this year and Mr. Doria have been condemned to pay indemnity to Sao Paulo Patrimonial Protection Funding – Funcap. This event is an example of how misjudgment can ruin a terrific artistic mural when authorities treat art as vandalism.

Summing up, Graffiti has emerged from a simple and innocent mark made by teenagers on walls to astonishing artistic works charged of purpose and meaning. This form of art may not be confused with vandalism, as it expresses human thoughts and desires through imagination, and requires skilled artists to translate feelings and social demands to terrific murals in urban environment, made by people to people.
