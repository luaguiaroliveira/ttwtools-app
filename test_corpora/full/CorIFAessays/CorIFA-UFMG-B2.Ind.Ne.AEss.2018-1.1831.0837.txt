<The Hydroelectric’s Impacts>

The hydroelectric is a construction that produces electric energy through the hydraulic potential existing in a river, in other words, generates electricity from the energy of the water, the hydropower. Consequently, efficiency is high and fuel cost is nil. However, as countries build more hydropower projects, the activity of damming rivers can result in impacts for natural resources, wildlife, society and Earth. In this way, hydroelectric have negative effects and sides.

The structure of a hydroelectric plant requires a large space, because it is necessary a reservoir that stores water, a dam that controls the flow and turbines, which are moved by the power of water and turn a generator to produce electricity in the plant. Consequently, damming a large part of a river can cause damage, for example, to extinguish habitats, animals and natural resources, to destroy the flora and riparian forests, to sand the riverbed and to reduce the oxygen in the water. According to studies in England’s University of East Anglia, Brazil’s Balbina Dam, built in 1986, has flooded an area of approximately 3,000km² and modified all the forest around it and turned it into an artificial archipelago, caused the majority of wildlife disappeared and increased emissions of greenhouse gases.

Moreover, dams also have social and cultural impacts, because it generates problems of reallocation of riverine populations, indigenous communities and small farmers, who need to change habits or lose these at all. According to National Geographic, the Chinese hydroelectric plant “The Three Gorges Dam” on Yangtze River displaced more than 1 million of people and flooded thousands of villages since the beginning of its construction in 1994. Furthermore, the “Belo Monte Power Plant” on Xingu River, which promises to be the largest in Brazil, is expected to be completed in 2019, but has already inundated many lands where indigenous groups have lived for centuries.

Therefore, the way a dam is operated can have different effects. In this way, projects to reduce the impacts of hydroelectric plants are necessary. According to LIHI, a company dedicated to reducing impacts generated by hydropower and non-profit, strategies to protect river flows, water quality, watersheds, endangered species such as the passage of fish and cultural processes, can be made through a control and be adhered. Another alternative for fish is its refueling. According to Fortum, a Finnish electricity company, it has been restocked more than one million fish in the water systems from Sweden and Finland and some habitats were restored. In addition, alternative energy sources should be considered. In Brazil, for example, the use of solar energy would be viable. As a result, minimizing impacts and studying the use of other renewable energies helps to reduce the impacts generated by hydropower.
