<Does the freedom of speech have limits?>

In August of 2017, the march of white supremacists in the city of Charlottesville in the USA resulted in 3 deaths. Cases such as this, in which intolerance appears camouflaged by the right of freedom of speech, gain repercussion, generating debates about the existence of limits of this constitutional guarantee. However, we must bear in mind that the relativization of this fundamental right can have serious consequences, as the censorship of thoughts that the ruling elite opposes.
It is noted, first, that the rule of law differentiates freedom of speech from hate speech. The STF - the supreme body of the judiciary responsible for keeping the Constitution - understood that the thought that is considered a tool for the propagation of violence should not be protected. While this fundamental right has a prevailing position in our law, it may be restricted in the name of combating prejudice and intolerance to stigmatized minorities. In this respect, the purpose of relativizing such a guarantee is to prevent intolerance. However, we must take into account the risks arising.
It should be noted that if freedom of expression has limits, these will be stipulated by some state body. That being so, we run the risk of the state being the dictator of morality and good manners. Such a situation is conducive to totalitarian governments such as North Korea, China, Venezuela, among others, because the state machine may understand that opposition to the government is a hate speech, thus generating a general repression of opponents, violating freedom of expression of the citizen. So we must exercise caution when restricting rights.
Therefore, people who rely on the state to regulate morale should be aware, because in a future change in social structures, control of freedom of expression can be used to silence these same people. That is why, a more effective way of suppressing violent ideologies (while these are only in words) would be the boycott promoted by organized civil society, rejecting institutions that disrespect human rights by cutting economic, political and personal relations.
