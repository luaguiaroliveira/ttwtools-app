The Information Literacy and the role of the librarian in mediation of information are discussed by academics and professionals involved. While the benefits for education or even leisure are undeniable, the professionals need to be ready to understand the information needs of the users to deliver them the best result as possible. This essay examines how the professional may train the users, leading to the desired information as efficient as possible.
The concept of Information Literacy, among others, involves the idea of information skill. Information competent users are more adapted to the research processes. For educational purposes, the librarians have a significant role in training the users to develop such skills. For doing so, the professionals must be proficient on them also. According to Kuhlthau (1996), highlights the significance on the mediation on the teaching and learning processes that happens through the information search and use. Her studies proved that users (high school students) that despite knowing their tasks and how their school library works, did not know the role of the librarian, wich was seen as a source locator and not a mediator, showed anxiety, confusion and frustration towards the library, the tasks and themselves. If they did know what to ask, as if the professionals knew how to train them, the situation would be different.
Based on that study, a similar situation was applied on undergraduate students of librarianship at Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais, and even the academics, inside the Escola de Ciencia da Informacao (Information Science School) had their difficulties for the same reason: lack of information literacy, as well as lack of knowledge of what are the roles of a reference librarian, and how could the professional help them on their findings. Information Literacy is more studied as a terminology than a educational paradigm. If it was taken more seriously, it would show the full potential of the librarian as a mediator and educational support for the students and the teachers. 
Librarians, when well trained and proficient with information literacy, are able to provide users with ability enough to be independent during research (learning to learn) and in various aspects of life.
