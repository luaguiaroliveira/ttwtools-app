It is well known that physical activities are essential for promote a general health avoiding many chronicle and non-chronicle diseases such as hypertension, coronary heart disease, stroke, diabetes, breast and colon cancer. It also reduces the risk of falls, promotes bone and functional health and is fundamental to energy balance and weight control (WHO, 2016). But what most people don’t know is about the mental benefits of physical activities.
According to World Health Organization (WHO) Mental health Atlas country profile (2014), in the United States of America 4% of the population has some serious mental disease registered. But as said by Fox (1999) There are the less severe problems that afflicts many other people and represents the biggest part of the health disease problem in population. Those are, for example: Depression (which can affect 5 to 10 percent of population in most developed countries), emotional distress, low self-esteem, poor body image, sense of hopelessness, chronic stress and anxiety.
This study from Dr Kenneth R Fox (1999) made on the Department of Exercise and Health Sciences of the University of Bristol, UK, was an over view on studies about this topic at the time and it showed how physical activities can be determinant on mental health as it is in physical health. According to him there is four ways the physical exercises can act on the psychological health: Treating mental, preventing of mental illness and disorders, improving mental and physical well-being of people with mental illness and Improvement of mental well-being of the general population.
Still on Fox (1999) study, it was shown that the physical activity can act on those mental disorders that afflict most part of the population. The evidences shows that the risk of clinical depression is decreased with physical activity, aerobic and resistance training has positive effects treating depression and the magnitude of this improvement can be compared to psychotherapeutic interventions as shown by experiment studies; It can provide reduces on anxiety and stress; It also has been shown that exercises promote a positive effect on your mood, As said by the author: “ <exercises makes you feel good> ”; Exercise can change people’s perceptions of their physical self and identity in a positive way; For the elderly, Physical activity can keep you alert and jovial; It also improve the sleep quality.
As we can see, the physical activity has many good effects in the physic an also mental health. For this reason a combination of cardiorespiratory exercise, resistance exercise, flexibility exercise and neuromotor exercise is highly recommended by the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM).
