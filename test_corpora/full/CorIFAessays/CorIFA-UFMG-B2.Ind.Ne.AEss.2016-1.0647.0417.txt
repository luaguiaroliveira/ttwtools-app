<The origin of Guitar>
	The guitar is one of the most famous musical instruments on world. It is very easy to find a guitar player; a great number of houses around the planet has one of these instruments inside, but not too easy is known where the guitar creation was. Musicologists are convinced that the recent development and consolidation of the current structure are made in the Iberia peninsula, what explains the common name Spanish Guitar, especially in the hand of the luthier Antonio de Torres Jurado (1817 – 1892).
	Bartoloni (2000) claims the origin of instruments with strings and sounding board are estimated about 5000 B.C. According to historian exist an illustration from 3500 B.C that shows an ancient shape of the guitar and Homero tells in the renowned “Odyssey” that Ulisses pull the string of his bow to produce a bright and vibrant note.
	Different hypothesis of guitar origin are raised beyond the history, although two versions are currently highlighted by researches like Pujol (1930). The first theory argues that the instrument in question is a development of the Arabian lute, which infiltrated in European culture in Moorish invasion of the Iberia peninsula (711). The other assumption says the beginning was correlated to the Roman zither, subsequent instrument of the extinct Greek khetara, who spread themselves during the Roman Empire (27 B.C. – 476).
	Azpiazu (1961) resumes the different speculation with argue about lute, vihuela and guitar genesis: “They are all from Arabic-Asian origin, the first of which received an Arabic name, the second a Roman name, and the third a Greco-Roman name”. It is knew they three coexisted with a few more instruments also similar aspect and structure like theorbo and mandolin, proven by illustration of the period, like “Cantigas de Santa Maria” (Afonso X, XIII Century), that shows they together and given different names to them similar to Moorish guitar or Latin guitar.
	The antique Greek mythology, in the myth of Hermes birth, says the god, right after leaving the cavern where he came to life, faced to tortoise shell, which he grabbed seven strings made by sheep’s gut, creating the first lira. This is probably the most near we can reach to the beginning of the guitar. Today we have a hundreds of variations to instruments similar to it and almost certainly they came to the same place lost in the history, when a man discover that a string can produce a sound and a shell, or a sounding board make by wood, can propagate. The coexistence of different kind of “guitars” around the world allows constantly development and propagation of the instrument.
