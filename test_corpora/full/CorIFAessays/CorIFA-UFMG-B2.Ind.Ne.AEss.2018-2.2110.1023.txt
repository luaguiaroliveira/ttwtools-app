Misrepresentation of LGBT+ people in movies has been a major issue since the past decades.  In 1930, the United States started to censure any movie that did not follow Hays Code, a document created to censure any kind of “obscenity”. For instance, movies with transgender, men or women kissing, having sex or even holding hands were censured by the Hays Code. In this essay, I contend that even though many movies are not under censorship in USA or Brazil, many productions still need to well-represent LGBT+ community, specially on movies.

After the expansion of cinema, radio and TV in Brazil, people started to watch or listen to shows that most of times prioritized what their telespectors did want to hear or see. Given that marginalized groups were not considered to have representation, they were shown on inferior positions or misrepresented by being a joke in the show. This stereotype perpetrated for many years in the imaginary of the audience that watched movies. Moreover, this idea about this group do not even created a conception that this people were jokes but also made them believe that there was not other representation for this group.

The idea of “acceptable representation” of LGBT+ people were very strong since the beginning of cinema expansion. Due to the idea that sex was also obscene, LGBT+ community were represented as unsexualitized, and even if two people of the same gender and/or sex held hands, they were seen as immoral. Consequently, the dissemination of this conception in society made this minority group suffer even more prejudice and violence, specially regarding sexual repression.

Since the beginning of the expansion of cinema, people believed cinema was a tool to either educate or persuade the audience. As a result, representation of “immorality” such as homossexuality, sex, and any kind of “obscenity” should not be shown to telespectors. Only after 1990, LGBT+ movies started to be less censored, specially in Brazil. Therefore, this 28 years of movie production were not enough to well-represent LGBT+ community. For that reason, many movies, soap operas and radio stations still need to represent this minority group in a way that they will not be seen as jokes, monsters or dangers to society but as human beings like everyone else.
