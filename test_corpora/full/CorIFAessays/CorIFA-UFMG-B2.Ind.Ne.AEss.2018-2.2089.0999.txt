<Bone graft in patients with cleft lip>

The cleft lip with fissure is the congenital face change with the highest worldwide incidence, affecting on in every 700 births (Dixon et al. 2011). It occurs early in pregnancy when the sides of the lip and the roof of the mouth do not fuse together as they should. A child can have cleft lip, cleft palate, or both. The most immediate concern for a baby with cleft palate is good nutrition. Sucking for children with a cleft palate is difficult because of the poorly formed roof of the mouth. It is also observed that factors directly affecting the psychological development of a child born with cleft lip and palate include possible speech and language disorders, facial disfigurement, and hearing loss (Richman at al. 1982). Surgeries should be done throughout the child's development to provide a better quality of life.
Psychological problems like lowered self esteem and difficulties in social interaction have also been noted in them. To treat the patient with cleft lips different areas are needed to help with the problems, surgeries are made during the development of the child, because the skills of different areas there may be many people involved as a plastic and craniofacial surgeon, pediatrician, dentist, speech specialist and a psychologist. Most people had positive experiences of the surgery they had received as children and teenagers and were satisfied with the outcome as far as their appearance was concerned. They also believed that the other treatments they had received from the cleft services, including speech and language therapy and psychological support, had helped them to feel more accepted by society.
The protocol of treatment is multidisciplinary and starts around 3 to 6 months of age with a labial repair, a palatoplasty is doing with 10 to 12 months, at the age of 9 to 12 years old a secondary alveolar bone grafting is indicated, when the canine has completed half to three quarters of its root formation. Orthodontic treatment before bone grafting might be performed, aiming to improve the maxillary relationship and the facial profile, leading to a more favorable graft prognosis. The bone grafting is a key part of the treatment.
Most people were satisfied with the outcome as far as their appearance was concerned. This is important to show how important the bone graft is for successful treatment.They also believed that the other treatments they had received, including speech and language therapy and psychological support, had helped them to feel more accepted by society.
To conclude, bone graft surgery is very important and must be performed, as well as all the surgeries performed at the right time, with psychological support and family support, lead the patient with cleft lip to a productive and happy life.
