In the XXI century, the world suffered transformations to attend the accelerated way of life imposed by capitalism and globalization; went through not only an intense process of industrialization but a high increase of the population, and nothing more appurtenant than the urbanization progress follow this tendency. In Brazil, specifically, this urbanization occurred belatedly, although in a faster way than its own planning, coefficient that was determinant to the current urban configuration of the country. 

Firstly, it is noticeable that the Brazilian urbanization process was in the past and still is excluding, once the urban area division occurs according to the social position of the individual, therefore, the availability of urban equipments for the citizens is determinated by the purchasing power of them. The result of this emplacement is the occurrence of a lot of “cities” condensed in the image of only one urban center, since people from different social classes live different realities. In addition, this socio-spatial segregation is not only physical but symbolic as well; there is prejudgment between lower classes and the south region just as upper classes and suburban areas. One example of this prejudgment is the “rolézinhos”, lower classes’ tours in southern malls that frighten residents of this region. 

Secondly, this situation of segregation is intensified by the shortage of public areas in the cities, in such a way that the harmonic interaction between classes is not promoted, and it turns even more recurrent the “camarotização*” of the public spaces. One example of this situation is the Mineirão stadium, located in Belo Horizonte; after constructions for the World Cup in 2014, its ticket office turned inaccessible to the lower classes. 

Finally, the increase in the mobility demand is not attended, once there was no investment in this aspect. This situation causes a considerable limitation in the possibilities of public transportation, especially between lower classes, and compromises the citizens’ right to come and go.

To summarize, it is necessary, by governmental or private initiative, the construction of public areas such as squares and theatres, with the intention of encourage healthy relations between social classes, as a way to decrease stress among them. Still talking about those initiatives, the investment in availability of urban equipments is highly demanded, such as feeding, leisure and cultural areas especially in suburban areas, aiming the creation of new centers. About mobility, the State should invest in a model of integration between the models of transportation, in a federal scale; road and metropolitan modal, for instance, in order to guarantee the right of come and go.
