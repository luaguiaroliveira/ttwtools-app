<Carbonaceous materials obtained by the CVD process for environmental applications>

The industrial development and growth of the world population created several environmental problems, one of these problems is the excessive generation of waste or contaminated, both in the industrial sector, such as pharmaceuticals and textiles, as in teaching and research and in homes. Thus, it became necessary to develop processes for the removal of these environmental contaminants.
Among these residues are emerging contaminants, which are defined as natural or synthetic substances that interfere with the endocrine system or a system biota. Antibiotics and hormones are in this class of emerging contaminants, considering the use of these compounds has increased greatly in recent decades.
The amount of antibiotics manufactured from 1940 increased significantly due to its wide use in humans, veterinary medicine and agroindustry (for example, control of pests and plants cloning). The release of these drugs in the environment can cause the bacteria resistant and physiological damage for the populations, even in low amounts. Already hormones once administrated, has released part of it by through the urine and/or faeces, in its active form, causing many environmental problems, such as feminization of male fish and infertility that environment.
The search for multifunctional materials has increased significantly, therefore, not all the desired characteristics for using them are found in a unique material. The organic-inorganic hybrid have complementary properties that are not found in a unique substance, which can be applied in several areas. Furthermore, these materials to be amphiphilics, have good dispersion in water due to its hydrophilic character and good interaction with organic compounds due to character hydrophobic.
Carbon is one the most versatile chemical elements in the periodic table, due to the type, strength and number of bond that can form with many different elements. The carbonaceous materials are available in a wide variety of forms including graphite, diamond, fullerene, carbon foam, activated carbon and carbon nanotubes.
Carbon nanotubes have been used to produce hybrid composites, which are used as part of the hydrophobic material and the matrix, which is used in the synthesis thereof, it act as a hydrophobic part. The carbon nanotubes has been investigate as potential adsorbents in removing contaminates. For example, one studied showed that single-way carbon nanotubes were able to remove phenanthrene, another study reported the competitive sorption of the pyrene, phenanthrene and naphthalene multiwall carbon nanotube.
These materials can be produced by different methods such as laser ablation, arc discharge and chemical vapor deposition (CVD). The laser ablation and arc discharge are able to produced either single-wall nanotubes and multi-walled nanotubes, but the former is unable to produce carbon nanotubes on a large scale, however the method discharge arc produced on a large scale nanotubes, but simultaneously is produced a large amount of carbon structure, amorphous carbon, being therefore necessary purification.
The CVD process is gaining importance and is considered the most practical and economical method for the production of carbon nanotubes on a large scale. Different compounds can be used as a carbon source for the synthesis of such nanostructures. CVD is a process in which one or more precursors are transported through the vapor phase to the reaction site, usually a tube within a furnace, which is heated over a Decomposes matrix.
For the formation of nanotubes, the presence of catalysts, generally transition metals, dispersed on the support is required. The catalyst system is prepared by precipitation of metal salts or impregnation of salts of the catalysts in the ceramic carrier, followed by calcination.
In the CVD carbon, there is the deposition of carbonaceous structures via catalytic decomposition of a volatile carbon source, on a thermally stable support with the formation of some gaseous by products that are expelled out of the tube. More used carbon sources include carbon monoxide, benzene, ethylene, methane and ethanol. The principal media used are silica, magnesium oxide (MgO) and alumina, as such materials and are thermally stable have large surface areas.
Given this context, it is concluded that the obtaining of carbon nanotubes through CVD process, suggests an alternative route organized as carbon nanotubes and staggered and are promising materials obtained in the removal of contaminants from aqueous solution emerging.
