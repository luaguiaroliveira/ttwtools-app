      Deviating from the conventional Regional Science approach, this essay synthesizes a theoretical framework to understand Urban Economics. My approach highlights the importance of land as an asset, the interdependence of individual decisions, and the preeminence of the urban capitalists to affect the dynamics of the city. The intellectual traditions that informs it are the Marxian land rent theory, the Post-Keynesian urban convention concept, and the Schumpeterian entrepreneur idea. This effort is heavily based on Abramo's (2007) pioneering – and widely unrecognized - work on theoretical Urban Economics. This theoretical exercise leads to an empirical econometric model, which is able to explain the changes in real estate prices after an intense State intervention, such as large-scale urban projects. Then, I use data from the Northern Vector of the Metropolitan Area of Belo Horizonte to test the hypothesis that land rent, urban convention and the behavior of entrepreneurs are essential dimensions of the urban economy dynamics.
On contrary of other areas within the economic science, such as Macroeconomics, the Urban Economics field has a less clear division between orthodox and heterodox approaches. The Regional Science attempt to insert the space into the neoclassical framework may certainly be interpreted as an orthodox approach to the matter. It is easy to realize by checking the fundamental hypothesis behind Regional Science's works: the belief on an equilibrium point, the homo economicus dogma that informs the individual maximization, the homogeneity of the agents, and the non-consideration of historical and cultural aspects (Abramo, 2007; Almeida, 2016; Martin, 1999). On the other hand, critical perspectives in general lies 'outside' Economics, within Economic Geography and Urban Planning, or more broadly, within the highly heterogeneous field of Urban Studies.
In this panorama, this essay is an attempt to synthesize a heterodox approach to Urban Economics. In others words, it is possible to say that this synthesis deviates from the Regional Science tradition, but it lies "inside" economic theory - drawing on land rent theory, on the Post-Keynesian notion of convention and on the Schumpeterian explanation for innovation. It is argue that the land rent theory is crucial to understand the nature of capitalist cities, and more specifically, the real estate prices. In addition, Abramo's (2007) effort to provide a coherent heterodox framework to understand the cities' dynamic makes the other two-third of the synthesis here. Abramo (2007) contributed to this challenge creating the concepts of urban convention and the urban entrepreneur. The first concept highlights the interdependence among individual decisions in the manner that they choose a place to live and to buy a real estate. The urban convention is a way to understand cities' spacial configuration and its patterns. The second concept gives the dynamic dimension to that configuration, emphasizing how new frontiers are open in the city and how the psychologically depreciation of real estate agglomerations occurs. The action of urban enterpreneurs, of the State and of the banks shapes new urban conventions, leading to change in the neighborhood's status - consequently, on the price and rent of the real estate located there.
	With this theoretical approach on hands, the construction of a model clarifies and exemplifies it. Then, an econometric model is derived from it as a heuristic tool able to capture the dynamics of a city or its portions. Finally, this model is tested with the data from Belo Horizonte's Northern Vector. This vector of the Metropolitan Region of Belo Horizonte (MABH) has been passing to a spatial restructuring, after a huge exogenous shock of public investment through the form of large-scale urban projects (LSUP). These LSUP might have affected the urban convention in the area, and through the action of urban enterpreneurs, might have change the land rent. Therefore, it is seen as an interesting site to test these formulations. Moreover, the MABH represents a very typical kind of Latin American metropolis, with a huge socio-spatial inequality and which undergone a rapid urbanization in the last 50 years.
The rest of the essay is structured as follows. The next section provides the theoretical discussion and ends with the econometric model. Section 3 presents data and methods used. Section 4 shows and discusses the results. Section 5 concludes emphasizing how the land value capture instruments may be useful to self-finance the urban infrastructure.
