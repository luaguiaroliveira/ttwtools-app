<BROMELAIN PURIFICATION BY MEMBRANE SEPARATION PROCESS>

The commercial production of bromelain, which is one enzyme with high added value, involves several steps and the purification processes are those whose operations are the most expensive, accounting for 70-90% of the total costs (SOARES et al., 2012). Many strategies have been used to purify bromelain, including ion exchange chromatography, precipitation and membrane processes. Membrane technology is developing at high speed and appears as an attractive method for the separation of enzymes (ZHANG et al., 2010). This essay will show the limitations of conventional processes and will demonstrate why membrane purification process is a good strategy for this type of separation.
Bromelain is a digestive protein that can be categorized as a protease, which means that it has the catalytic capacity of hydrolyzing peptide bonds of proteins (TAP et al., 2016). Due this function, bromelain exhibit unique applications for the food, pharmaceutical, cosmetic and textile industries (NOR et al., 2016; WU et al., 2017). Moreover, it is extracted from pineapple and can be obtained even from its inedible parts, usually considered as waste (NOR et al., 2016). Because that, a lot of process have been studied to purify this enzyme.
In comparison with other methods, ion-exchange chromatography is known to have high specificity; however, some limitations restrict its use on industrial scale as high cost of the technique and low processing capacity (YIN et al., 2011; COSTA et al., 2014).  On the other hand, the precipitation method generate highly concentrated products, are easy to scale (SILVESTRE et al., 2012) and involve simple equipment (SILVA et al. 2006), but the main problem of this method is the use of high concentrations of salts and solvents that, in many cases, can not be discarded in the environment, which implies additional steps of recovery (CAMPOS et al., 2017). 
In this context, the membrane process arise as an attractive purification technology, since it offers great advantages such as high product yield, easy scale-up,  environmentally friendly and economical (NOR et al., 2016). In addition, it is easy to operate, selective, can be applied in the separation of thermolabile (LIMA et al., 2009) and produce less waste (NOR et al., 2015). A study about the introduction of membrane process reported a substantial increase of 6.5 to 8.5 in bromelain concentration (NOR et al., 2017). It is also important to highlight that membrane process occur in mild conditions, which is a huge superiority, because enzymes are very sensible to extreme operational conditions. 
In conclusion, the study of alternative purification methods, such as membrane process, which is aimed at obtaining more viable processes, are of great interest. In fact, membrane process presents huge advantages over traditional technologies, as it was presented. Bromelain is an enzyme with many applications and because it can be obtained from pineapple waste, its purification has been studied a lot. Membrane process show special features mainly when it comes to enzymes, since they are sensitive to extreme conditions.
