<Dental care at the Unified Health System: a great advance in the Brazilian public health>

The inclusion of dental care in the Brazilian National Health System (SUS) represented an expansion in the dental care access to the Brazilian population. Also, represented an expansion in the variety of dental procedures performed in the primary health care, access to secondary care, improvement of the dental facilities and a better qualification of dentists. Currently, primary and secondary care in dentistry at SUS includes a large number of dental procedures and this has been impacting the oral health condition of the population. Therefore, this essay will discuss the importance of dental care inclusion at SUS to the improvement of Brazilian population health.
The Brazilian Unified Health System was created in the late 1980, after an important negotiation process between the population and the government. It was based on the Brazilian Constitution of 1988, that says “ <Health is a right of everyone and a duty of the State> ”, being present since the primary care attention to organ transplantation. The Health Family Strategy, created in 1994, is the primary health care organization of SUS, and a mark of the expansion to the health care access. The inclusion of dental care into SUS occurred in 2001 once, until then, just had access to dental treatment people with social security and children under school.
However, only after the National Oral Health Policy of 2003 the number of dental facilities and oral health teams increased considerably, resulting in the expansion of dental care access by the Brazilian population. Since then, the number of dental procedures offered increased, including preventive, restorative, surgical and endodontic procedures, oral cancer monitoring and provision of dental prostheses. It resulted in an improvement of the oral health condition of Brazilian population, especially children under 5 years, according to the last Brazilian oral health survey. However, it still necessary to expand the provision of dental prostheses, once the elderly received a very limited and non-conservative treatment in the past, resulting in a high rate of edentulism. Also, dental carie and periodontitis still being the biggest problem among adolescents and adults, which means that the PHC services cannot embrace all the Brazilian population.
The inclusion of OHT at SUS represented a great advance in the PHC, expanding the access to all Brazilian population and expanding the variety of dental procedures offered. Although the Brazilian dental health condition had improved in the past two decades, still need to improve more, especially in dental rehabilitation.
