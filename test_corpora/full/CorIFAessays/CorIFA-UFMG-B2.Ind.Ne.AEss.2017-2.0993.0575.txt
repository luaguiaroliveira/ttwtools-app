Inclusive Education is one of the great challenges of today, being a field of struggles and tensions. In Brazil, the struggle for equity in the right to education – regarding subjects with special needs – can be traced back to the promulgation of the Federal Constitution in 1988, in which education is affirmed as a right of all. It can be summarized as the process of allowing all children to have the opportunity to participate fully in regular classroom activities, regardless of disability, race or other characteristics. In this essay, I will argue that, for inclusion to be effective, it is up to the educator to take a fresh look at the individual differences of each student, as well as the pedagogical implications on their teaching practices.

Historically speaking, the school institution has difficulty coping with diversity. This, coupled with the fact that the individual teachers are not professionals detached from their own concepts and preconceptions, makes the recognition of multiple "identities" in school spaces present as a problem that the professional teacher faces daily. Whether it is the badness of the labeling, the complexity of the medical reports, or the particularity that each deficiency brings with it, the fact is that when the teacher faces difficulty teaching a child other than the so-called "normal", he is usually paralyzed in his educational task, which is reflected directly by the students involved, who are often excluded from the possibilities of learning.

It is in this sense that an inclusive educational practice presupposes more than the desire to live daily with students considered deficient or different, but a commitment to the responsibility of the profession itself, to educate all, indistinctly. The inclusive teacher does not allow himself to be blocked by difficulties, but advocates alternatives that guarantee not only the formal education of his students but also informal education - that which forms for life. He thinks and (re)thinks of pedagogical activities that are directed to his audience, considering the students' experiences, their previous knowledge, their limits and/or possibilities. By taking advantage of the themes that pulsate in the context of the classroom, he favors the construction of contents and scientific truths, without forgetting to predict the human formation of these individuals.

In conclusion, an education that has as premise the inclusion of all subjects recognizes the need for a pedagogical practice that perceives and values all the scenarios and educational spheres in which people are involved. Thus, individuals are not reduced to mere instruments or "reservoirs" of content, but highlighted as moral subjects, diverse and diversified, actors in their own realities and valued in their differences.
