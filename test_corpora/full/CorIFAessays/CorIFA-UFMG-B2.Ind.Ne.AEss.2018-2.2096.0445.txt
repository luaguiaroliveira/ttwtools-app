<Developmental Dyslexia and its symptoms>

Developmental Dyslexia (DD) is a condition characterized by the deficit in the ability to decode letter in sounds which leads to slow and inaccurate recognition of words (avila, Carvalho & Kila, 2009; Dehaene, 2012; Pinheiro & Scliar-Cabral, in press; Scliar-Cabral, 2013). This disability has the higher prevalence of all Specific Learning Disorder (SLD) [DSM-5, 2013]. In Brazil, DD is founded in more than 10% of the population (Gutierrez & Tomasi, 2001). Therefore many students could have it, making essential to comprehend how to identify them. This essay displays about three main criteria to diagnose: (a) specific school problem which is persistent and significant even with the extra effort, (b) average or above average intelligence, (c) seems like an unexpected underachievement. 
The manuals and experts agree that the Specific Learning Disorders are classified as particular school problems that appear in the early stages of development and education. Can be characterized by inefficiency and inaccuracy of the perception and processing of verbal and non-verbal information generating persistent and significant difficulties in educational performance in one or more school areas ₋ reading, writing, spelling, reasoning, arithmetic. Thoughtful the level of performance by age and schooling, low academic achievement remains (DSM-5, 2013), even with extraordinarily high levels of effort or support (CID 10, 2006; NJCLD, 2011; National Information Center for Children and Youth with Disabilities -NICHCY, 2011; DSM-5, 2013; GreatSchool, 2016).
Studies show that children with specific learning disorders have average or above average intelligence. The clinical picture is explained by cognitive problems linked to metacognition, perception, spoken language, attention, an organization of thought, cognitive flexibility, motor planning, and social interaction. The deficits are intrinsic to the individual, therefore, of neurobiological basis (interaction between genetic, epigenetic and environmental factors) lifetime. There is variation between people in gravity and the number of signs and symptoms, being more common in boys than Girls (War, 2002; CID 10, 2006; Smith & Strick, 2007; Zorzi, 2010; NJCLD, 2011; NICHCY, 2011; DSM-5, 2013; GreatSchool, 2016). 
The main sign that there is a disturbance of this nature is the perception that the child is always below the expected for their capabilities (Unexpected underachievement). The underlying neurological changes affect learning skills that do not emerge from biological maturation (requiring explicit teaching), so the framework is called specific learning disorder (NJCLD, 2011; DSM-5, 2013; GreatSchool, 2016)
Due to the overlap of symptoms with other specific disorders such as DDA, ADHD, differential diagnosis is difficult. When they are not correctly diagnosed and treated, dyslexic children can develop problems of adaptation, evasion of the school and adopt antisocial behaviors (Pinheiro et al., 2012), making the early identification essential (Scarborough, 1990; Catts, 2002).
