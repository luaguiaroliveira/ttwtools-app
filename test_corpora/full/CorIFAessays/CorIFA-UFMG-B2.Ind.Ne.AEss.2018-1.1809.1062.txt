<The dangers of unlimited access to information>

In modern society, with internet access and countless social media platforms, we have almost unlimited access to information. So much that we all also become source of information as we easily pass ahead the news we have to share. However, how can we filter all the information we access and decide what is reliable? In order to reach the truth readers must be careful with the traps that could mislead and leave us less informed than we were before. 
Now days it is possible to say that we are all potential reporters, with a few clicks we share information about events that happen around us with anyone who is interested to know. On the other hand, if everyone is a potential reporter maybe no one really is one. Opinions and facts may change from one person to another and if you only hear one point of view you might not get the whole story.
Another problem rises even if you check different outlets and diverse points of view of a story you might get so many contradictory details and still not know in what to believe. Still, it is important to check all these views, then turn them into a sort of puzzle. This way we can compare what they share in common and put the pieces together on our own to reach an impartial version of the story before deciding which side we want to take. At least you can form your own opinion instead of being blinded by one side only.
The most chaotic the situation the more important it is to check for the latest updates through several moments of the day, instead of every few minutes. It is crucial to give time for complete detail to emerge to and false ones to be refuted. During events such as natural disasters or public attacks it is easy to get anxious but spreading false rumors will only lead to more panic or wrongful accusation. 
Modern world gave us the gift of unlimited access to knowledge and information but we must be careful not to let this gift become our curse. Today we are free of the old media gatekeepers that used to control our information but it does not mean we should not control the flow of information ourselves. Freedom demands responsibility, otherwise it becomes more prejudicial than beneficial.
