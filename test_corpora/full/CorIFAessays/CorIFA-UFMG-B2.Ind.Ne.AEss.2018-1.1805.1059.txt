<The importance of the tolerance in the formation of the UN on the international system of the twentieth century>

Considering the international context from a historical perspective, as well as a certain fundamental theoretical concept, we have the formation of the UN as the expression of an emerging solidarity in the world. Funded in october 1945, the United Nations represent an effort by states to pacify global order. the purpose of this essay is to discuss the importance of tolerance in a international sphere and how such value represents a fundamental element in the context of the twentieth century for the formation and maintenance of the United Nations.
First, the tolerance was an important concept for the historical time of the creation of the UN. The world existing in the period after World War II was characterized by instability, uncertainty and a great lack of humanity, so a more peaceful and diplomatic international order was needed. Armed conflict should be largely avoided, as the world had just emerged from one of the greatest genocides in history. Thus, for the international anarchic environment and for the construction of an organ that sought a more peaceful solution of conflicts, tolerance was key.
Second, tolerance in this context was conducive to strengthening both the State internally and vis-à-vis other States at the international level. Internally, the state strengthened itself by promoting a decrease in violence and fostering greater solidarity, thus giving rise to the paradigm of the Democratic State of Law, which sought greater solidarity and fraternity. This international cooperation reflected in a strengthening of sovereignty over other states and an increase in sociability and transnationalism, improving foreign relations and internal relations.
Finally, tolerance came to be reaffirmed and brought about a strengthening of the UN structure with the 1948 Declaration of Human Rights. The declaration was an important legal achievement as it opposed the massacres of war and guaranteed to societies around the world the possibility of a greater plurality and respect for individuality. To those who had no guarantee of rights were guaranteed fundamental rights inherent to the human dignity of each one and all this related to the existing change in the state models themselves, reinforcing solidarity and fraternity.
In conclusion, tolerance after World War II was a fundamental aspect for the construction and maintenance of the UN and the international order itself. All ideals culminated in a change in the state model and in legal guarantees, providing a more tolerant and supportive international society.
