<Embrionic stem cell and reserch>

Stem cells can be defined by two properties: their ability to divide themselves in identical copies (self-renew) and their ability to differentiate themselves in others cells (differentiation). Due to these properties, they are an interesting cells source to use on cellular therapy and regenerative medicine. Stem cells are divided in two main groups: embryonic and adult. Among these, the embryonic stem cell has the highest differentiation capacity, and therefore are capable of differentiating into all tissues of the body. Obtaining embryonic stem cells necessarily involves the destruction of the embryo. While some cultures consider that the embryo only has the status of "life" when born, others consider it from fertilization of the embryo, and then prohibited the use of these cells in research. The research with embryonic stem cells are able to generate uncountable benefits for the society, so this should be allowed in every country.
Adult stem cells are already widely used in research and its applications in the clinic are diverse. As an illustration, we can cite bone marrow transplantation that a patient receiving blood stem cells from a donor. Thus, the use of embryonic stem cells could have much larger impacts, one that these cells have a greater ability to differentiating.
Following the advancement of medicine, organ transplants have become a reliable and practical everyday save many patients. However, the number of patients in need of organs is much greater than the amount of donors, which becomes a major problem. In this way, the stem cells present as a potentially unlimited source of tissue for transplantation. For example, there are already numerous studies using synthetic skin grafts and showing excellent results.
In countries where there is release of research with cells embryonic stem cells are obtained from IVF surplus embryos. In assisted reproduction, embryos are generated in the laboratory and a part of them implanted in the patient, the rest is frozen with funding from parents or discarded. This practice is carried out since 1978, but only twenty or thirty years later there was release of the use of embryos to obtain stem cells.
With the emergence of new diseases, and the advancement of technology, we need new tools for researchers to provide the quality of life society that medicine is able. One of these tools are the embryonic stem cells.
