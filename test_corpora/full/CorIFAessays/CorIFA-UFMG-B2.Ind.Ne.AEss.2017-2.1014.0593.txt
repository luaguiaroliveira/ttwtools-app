<International Remittances and Development>

Remittances of international migrants are becoming an important variable to underdeveloped countries, since they impact the livelihood of families and the national accounts. Some authors as Hein de Haas claim that these financial influxes are playing an important role towards development, looking to the increase presented from 2 billion US$ in 1970, to 71 bilion US$ in 2001 (Gammeloft, 2002). Although this measurement is not able to collect the total amount because of the fact that most transactions are made through informal channels, this already transmits the importance of the remittances.

There are two aspects of the impacts of remittances to underdeveloped countries: The microeconomic and the macroeconomic. In the microeconomic level, remittances signify the main connection between the migrant and its hometown. The majority of migrants saves loans in order to send to families and to complete the familiar earnings. In their turn, most of times, families need these deposits to bear basic expenses, due to the lack of opportunities at the hometown. Hence, the stocks of trade and investments at these places starts to depend highly on the financial influxes originated abroad. In the macroeconomic level, the remittances affect directly the foreign exchange, causing an appreciation of the national currency and consequently contributing to a better result to the balance of payments.

Another interesting point to consider about international remittances is that they are less volatile than other international capital flows, such as FDI (Foreign Direct Investments) and development aid (provided to underdeveloped countries by institutions as World Bank) and this happens due to the economic performance of the place they were sent from, a developed economy. Consequently, remittances have an anticyclical effect at the places they arrive. In other words, in a crisis moment, remittances play a more important role in sustaining the level of economic activity than the other forms of capital flows, helping this place to recover of the reverses consequences of the crisis.

One negative aspect of remittances is that sometimes the governments are not able to plan public policies to use them for a specific purpose. However, there are some measures that could help public administration to exploit the potential of these capital flows, as the one taken by Colombia's government. They made a Real Estate Fair to Colombians at Spain, in a partnership with the Colombia construction companies. The objective was to facilitate the investment of their emigrants in the national program of housing.

According to the exposed above, international remittances can signify opportunity of growth and development to the level of families and to the national level. The major part of the time, the immigrants sending cities are poor and presents few opportunities to the people, and the remittances represents reliefs on the account. But it is important that governments plan how remittances will be used, in a way that this capital flows can supply the main needs of the community and the country.
