<The construction and retraction of women in Alfred Hitchcock films>
It’s not easy being a woman in an Alfred Hitchcock film. Whether they’re being dive-bombed by birds, splattered at the base of a bell tower, or attacked by cross-dressing maniacs with mother issues, the master of suspense could be counted on to put his female characters through the proverbial ringer. This essay aims to characterize and analyze the way Hitchcock constructs and portrays women in his films.
Hitchcock is famous for have a female archetype in his movies a prim blond in a very stiff, austere suit who seems very clean pressed and proper on the outside but is a seething sexual cauldron on the inside, like Tippe Hedren show in The Birds and Marnie and Kin Novak in Vertigo. And the humorous, cultured, sophisticated, charming blond character portrait by Grace Kelly in Rear Window and To Catch a Thief.
Hitchcock’s leading ladies were often aroused by the danger brought about by their leading men. Manipulative, passionate and sometimes criminal, his heroines were richly complex characters infused with a deep sensuality that more often than not complicated the lives of Hitchcock’s heroes.
Finally, not every of his pictures have a blonde women star – Ingrid Bergman in Notorious and Spellbound is the biggest example. His woman’s are as beautiful as they are mysterious and contradictory, helping shape the pictures into masterpieces of suspense.
