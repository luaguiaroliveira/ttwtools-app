<Overlooks can be the reason of dengue growth>

Dengue is a viral disease that is transmitted by Aedes aegypti mosquito and represents a major global human concern. The disease is caused by dengue virus (DENV), an enveloped RNA virus which belongs to the family of flaviviridae. During the last three decades, there has been a global increase in incidence of dengue fever and dengue hemorrhagic fever. According to World Health Organization (2011), two fifths of the world’s population in tropical and subtropical countries are at risk in addition to an estimated 50 million dengue infections occur worldwide annually. Some factors such as the difficulty for vaccine production, the research investments neglected and the growth of mosquitoes in urban areas contribute to the increase of the disease.
The DENV occur in four closely related but antigenically and genetically distinct serotypes (Weaver and Vasilakis 2009), and discovering an accessible vaccine is an urgent need. Currently there is only one vaccine (Dengvaxia) available (limited to a few countries) on the market since 2015 after half a century’s intensive efforts (Gosh and Dar 2015). The development of an effective and accessible vaccine has major limitations as the difficulty of producing an efficient and safe vaccine against the four serotypes that can cause disease.
Dengue is a neglected disease in underdeveloped countries and present reduced investments in research, production of medicines and their control. Brazilian government investments should be increasing for both mosquito control and discovery of vaccines. The emergence of new viruses such as Zika as Chikungunya fever led to an in funding for research.
The number of A. aegypti mosquitoes is growing rapidly in urban areas. The large number of mosquitoes must be attributed as a consequence of globalization, unplanned and unregulated urban development, improper water storage, unsatisfactory sanitary conditions and global warming (Chaturvedi and Nagar 2008). A. aegypti mosquitoes also transmit Zika virus and Chikungunya virus which has spread to many countries and territories. One considerable problem for mosquito control in Brazil is due the occurrence of campaigns only in warm season of the year, and the eggs can resist for one year, thus is it impossible to exterminate it without a social and political mobilization during the whole year.
In conclusion, is noted that dengue is a public health problem in underdeveloped countries. The government of the countries in tropic and subtropics must create effective preventive strategies to control the mosquitoes and invest in research, both for the discovery of the vaccine or strategies for mosquito control.
