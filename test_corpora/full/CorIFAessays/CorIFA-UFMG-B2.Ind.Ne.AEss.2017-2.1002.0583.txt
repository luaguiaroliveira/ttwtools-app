<Should Samarco return mining operations in Mariana?>

A collapse of a mine tailings dam - Samarco property - occurred in Mariana in late 2015. Samarco was one of the biggest iron ore mining in Brazil and synonymous of technique. The tragedy killed 12 people and dropped around 62m3 of water and tailings mountainside, in an inhabited area [1] [2]. The tailings flooded one of the most important Brazilian rivers, killing aquatic life and making water undrinkable for hundreds of kilometers away. This is called the worst environmental tragedy in Brazilian history, and let behind a high human and environmental cost. Since there, Samarco stopped all mining operations and started a lot of projects and actions with the goal of recovering and compensation of environmental and social damages. Now, Samarco is being prevented by political issues from returning mining operations in Minas Gerais.
In October 2016, all Samarco operational licenses were suspended. For reviewing the licenses the state of Minas Gerais requests a Corrective Operational License (COL). Before requesting this COL Samarco must present compliance letters from all cities in its direct area of influence, that are Santa Bárbara, Mariana, Ouro Preto, Catas Altas and Matipó. All cities provided these letter, except for Santa Barbara. The doubt about the return seems only to aggravate the social and economic impacts of the tragedy [3].
The maintenance of Samarco standstill is causing financial problems for the surrounding mining areas. Samarco incomes represents 1,5% of Minas Gerais GPD and 6,4% of Espírito Santo GDP. The enterprise also they employees about 20 thousand direct and indirect jobs in two Brazilian states representing an income of approximately R$ 1,2 Bi [2]. This amount of money is also responsible for supplying local trade and boost and promote the region's economy. Also, until March, 2017, Samarco have dismissed around 40% of their employees [3]. If they do not return miming operations, this number tends to increase. The third reason is that the enterprise has no more own cash. Without money, it will be impossible to maintain the current staff and to continue the projects for compensating the caused damages [4]. These three reasons explain why it is important to overcome political issues that are preventing the returning of Samarco operations and making worse the social consequences of this sad tragedy.
Given this scenario and all mitigation and compensation projects already taken or underway, it seems the return of Samarco operations is of great importance for society and economy of the states of Minas Gerais and Espírito Santo. It is also of great importance that the enterprise continue to fulfill its commitments to social and environmental repair and ensure a safe and sustainable operation.
