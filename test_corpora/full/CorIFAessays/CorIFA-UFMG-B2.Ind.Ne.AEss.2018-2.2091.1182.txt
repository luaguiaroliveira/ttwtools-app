<Are we too clean>

In 1989 Strachan formulated the hygiene hypothesis, based in epidemiologic data he proposed that the comfort provided by the lifestyle that has been experienced in modern society such as reduced physical activity practice, a diet poor in fibbers and rich in highly processed and sugary food with preservative and other chemicals allied with the reduced contact with microorganisms during childhood, causes changes on human microbiota and, by consequence, increased number of autoimmune diseases and allergies. Here will be discussed the importance of microbiotic colonization in order to have a healthier immune system. 
Most of the data presented in the literature suggests that the gastrointestinal tract of a normal fetus is sterile and the contact with the mother’s birth canal and the milk prevenient from breastfeeding is essential in order to colonize the infant’s body and stimulate a proper development of the immune system. To prove that, several studies in germ free and specific pathogen free mice have been released lately. For example, a review from nature published by Jean-Francois Bach shows that in 90% of Specific Pathogen Free female and 20% to 40% male mice develop insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus and these rates decrease considerably when those mice are not in SPF condition. 
To add those evidences there are a group of microorganisms known as probiotics that can help to improve health by restoring the balance of the gut microbiota. They have been proposed to be used as treatment to many diseases such as diarrheal disorders, constipation, bowel and respiratory diseases and lactase deficiency.  Those microorganisms can be easily found in communities on many fermented food like yogurt, kefir and kimchi or isolated on pills.  
In conclusion the literature existent in immunology and microbiology have been demonstrating the connection between the microbiome in and on human body to autoimmune diseases and allergies. However many studies still need to be done to better understand how microorganisms affect the human body, mainly the immune system and the better way to use those microorganisms to benefit human health.
