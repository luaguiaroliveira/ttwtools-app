The phenomenon of the globalization has exerted pressure to the development of the language skills by ungraduated students in countries where English is not the official language. In fact, it is perceptible an increase in the use of non-native languages (mainly the English) in scientific publications even in areas that do not have this academic culture, like geographic sciences (Short, Boniche, Kim, & Li, 2001). This is already a reality in physical and biological sciences in the way that the most impacting scientific journals in these areas are edited in English (Garfield, 2006). Thus, improve language skills seems to be an interesting strategy for ungraduated students optimize his academics period.
This becomes even clearer when analyzed some numbers associated with the academic environment. One of the most present objects in this domain are books, which are, even in a connected world, a powerful tool to consolidate knowledge. However, the number of book publications in Portuguese language represents only about 1% of the total number of publications in the world, against about 25% of the English language (Lowden 2008). This suggests a significant increase in the information availability for bilingual students. This skill is useful also for classes in English. In fact, in top universities “ <the presence of foreign teachers, in transit or for long periods, is recurrent> ” as say professor Eduardo Viana Vargas, research at UFMG.
Furthermore, the benefits of study a new language are not restricted to the ungraduated period. The job market has demonstrated your preference to bilingual workers, reflecting even in the salary, which bilingual professionals gaining about 60% more. If the student chose to continue in the academic field, he will note already in the selection process (for master or doctorate programs) the importance of non-native languages.
In this sense, the increasing of the foreign languages in all possible professional environments leads to the valorization of bilingual workers. To prevent some penalty associated to this reality, is extremely important to the students to be prepared when they enter in the job market. Fortunately, opportunities to raise the language skills are common in top universities. UFMG for example, offers a range of classes in English for different finalities, like IFA (to improve the academic English skills) and others general English curses.
