<STEEL ANGLE SECTION IN ENGINEERING>

The angle section is an L-shaped type of cross section of steel products. It consists of two legs of equal or unequal length, with 90 degrees between them. These sections are often used with two angles placed back to back and connected by bolts or welds. It is important that the two single sections are tied to prevent them from buckle individually. The distance between the angles can be defined by the thickness of the plates.
The manufacturing process can be cold formed when the conformation process takes place at room temperature. If the process involves rolling the steel above the recrystallization temperature, it is called hot rolled. Cold formed steel is more expensive than hot rolled but also has less residual stress. Each case is approached differently as the process directly influences the section properties. 
Countless steel constructions use angle sections. According to Junyor (2019), this is due to a high structural efficiency, an elevated strength and a low weight. He also describes this type of section as being easier to produce and to connect, being widely used in buildings, sheds and bracing structures.
Generally, the angle section in traction is tied by one leg, as in transmission towers and truss systems. Vasconcelos et al (2005) explain that these eccentric connections, which not all members are attached, lead to irregular stress distributions in the bonded region. Therefore, the loads tend to concentrate more on the connected elements than at the untied ones. This effect is known as shear lag and reduces the ultimate strength of the element. 
Other factors influence the allowable tensile strength, for example, whether the connections are bolted or welded. When bolted, the opening must be removed from the gross area, defining the net area. The effective net area is the one that takes into account these two previous facts: the presence of holes and the eccentricity of the connection.
Despite its apparent simplicity and ease of use, angle analysis and sizing are not simple.  The structural steel construction design usually follows country specifications and the enounced definitions are accounted differently in each code. Hence, the same angle section designed with the Brazilian, American and European codes can provide different results.
Vasconcelos et al (2005) did a comparative study about angle section samples in tensile. When the number of fasteners is ranging from 3 to 7, the American code provides higher results for the effective net area than Brazilian code. On the other hand, they argue that if the number of bolts is fixed at 4 and the pitches vary, the European code provides results closer to numerical analyses than those of the effective net area the American and Brazilian code. In conclusion, the pratical results for the codes are substantially different from the experimental ones. 
Alternatively, the angle section can be subjected to compression load and buckle. Buckling is a type of structural failure represented by a sudden lateral deflection of a structural member. It is interesting to note that the failure is characterized by instability instead of ruptures or yielding of the material, which regularly occurs. Branquinho (2017) describes that hot rolled angle sections in compression generally buckles by bending rather than torsion or by the combined effects of bending and torsion.
To measure the propensity of a column to buckle, slenderness is defined. It is the quotient between the length and the radius of gyration. Ditz and Sieg (2016) states that members with low slenderness maintain a higher final load. In a general case, the reference codes tend to converge and the slenderness should not exceed 300 for tensile members and 200 for compression.
Furthermore, it is important the definition of the eccentricity of the load. Junyor (2019) reports that when the angle is under concentric compression, the codes and the numerical analysis converge. However, when the load is eccentric, none of the procedures did a relevant representation of the behavior of the angle section in compression. Junyor’s (2019) thesis creates a new proposal to represent this behavior and suggests that other authors do more experimental studies to confirm his results.
In summary, sometimes design results following code prescriptions, can be questionable and unrealistic. Obviously, as there as many variables that can influence the ultimate strength of a steel angle section, it is necessary to perform numerical and experimental analysis of this type of section On the other hand, the resources of money and time to do so are rarely available. 
For this reason, the structural engineer is responsible for the best decision. Valuable design is achieved when analytical thinking and problem solving skills are used, as well as technology and software, to study the best solution for each case. By inspecting construction conditions and the loads possibilities, it leads to safe design and construction, along with the best resources of money and time.
