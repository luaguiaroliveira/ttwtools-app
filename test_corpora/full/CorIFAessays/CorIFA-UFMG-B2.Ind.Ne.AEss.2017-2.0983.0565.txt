<Relationship between exercise and injury prevention>

It is well known the importance of have good habits of health, such as good alimentation and the regular practice of exercises. For athletes, this is not different and these exercises are very important to maintain their muscle skeletal system far away from injury. There are many prospective studies that proved the influence of have healthy muscles in injury prevention in athletes (Zazulak, Hewett e Reeves, 2007; Neumann, 2010). This essay aims to understand the importance of do specific exercises to prevent injury in athletes, to explain the muscle capacity-demand and finally to show the costs reduce to sport clubs when they have a good prevention program.

First of all there are strong arguments that human body is completely connected. The movement of proximal joints and segments can influence distal joints such as the shoulder and elbow or hip, knee and ankle, and consequently influence the performance an any activity (Leetun et al, 2004; Dierks et al, 2008). When athletes have core stability, since this is the center of the body, also other parts would be stable. Furthermore, the type of exercise performed depends on the type of activity or sports the athlete does. This is the training specificity. For example, runners need to practice more exercise to strengthen the core and inferior limbs muscles and throwing athletes should training superior limbs.

From this it is necessary have a good comprehension of muscle capacity-demand. This term is very popular in rehabilitation and describe the capability of the muscle skeletal system to respond a determined activity request (Fonseca et al., 2007). For example, when athletes have healthy muscles they can performance an action with low demand. This have an importance implication in injury prevention because strong muscles with a good flexibility could transfer energy to tendons, ligaments and capsules. Hence these structures could receive more load with no damaged (Leetun et al, 2004).

Finally, prevention is a crucial field of action for the sports health team, since it reduces the cost of emergency care, rehabilitation and the return of the athlete to the activity. In addition, athletes become less prone to withdrawal when they have a good ability to respond to imposed demands, resulting in less burden on the sport (Silva et al., 2013).

In conclusion, implementation of injury prevention programs, such as doing specific exercises, leads to reduction of staff costs and also health promotion for the athlete population.
