In the history of medicine, many researches have brought significant impacts in the treatments of diseases, especially the one made by Edward Jenner. He was the first to produce a vaccine to combat smallpox. This achievement provided the eradication of smallpox, improved the prophylaxis of many infectious diseases in addition to begin the study of immunology.
It is known that smallpox was the disease which killed most human beings since the beginning of the specie. Alongside bubonic plague and malaria, these pathologies became famous as the “black deaths”. Smallpox is an acute and contagious disease caused by variola virus. It was declared eradicated after a global immunization promoted by World Health Organization (WHO), a milestone that can be understood as one of the biggest advances in modern medicine once there is no other eradicated disease.
After the eradication of infectious forms in humans, the concern about remaining stocks of the virus increased, since they could be used as biological warfare. The decision taken by mutual agreement between countries was to eliminate all living forms of the virus, keeping some for exclusive research use.
Edward Jenner was an english physician and scientist, known by the production of the world’s first vaccine. He studied for some years the correlation between human and cow smallpox (cowpox), and observed people who had been exposed to the cow smallpox did not die from human smallpox when contaminated. In this context, Jenner demonstrated the protective effects of cowpox against smallpox.
Therefore, the vaccine is made by a weakened cowpox virus, which, mediated by the human immunologic system, confers the immunization against smallpox. The term vaccine was created based on the French word “vache” which means cow. Jenner is considered, nowadays, as the father of immunology because he was the pioneer of all vaccines used today against various types of illness, in addition to provide a beginning of the study of all of human being’s immune cells.
