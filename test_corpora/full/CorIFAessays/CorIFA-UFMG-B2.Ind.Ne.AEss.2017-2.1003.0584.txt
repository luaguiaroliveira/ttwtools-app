<When less is more: why parents should choose having just one child?>

When you decide how much children you are willing to have, it is important to remember that you have to dedicate part of your life to sustain your daughters/sons. In the past, when the system was based on agriculture and farming, having more children was a good choice because they were part of the family labor force. In this context more children led to more production and more prosperity. However, as the world changed to an industrialized system, and kids started to go to school instead of helping at household activity, everything changed. Nowadays children are synonymous of expense, because parents have to dedicate time to raise them and also spend money with their schools and all the goods necessary to their development. Since the world changed, it is more advantage to choosing having just one child.

One important aspect to consider is that the quality of time spent with just one child is better than when parents have to share their attention with several kids. One child requires less working and attention from the parents. According to a research made in University of Texas in 2015, the attention provided to parents to their child can lead to a better academic performance, an a better successful in work. Besides, this special attention can respond to a better confidence and emotional security to the child. A recent study from Southwest University, in Chongqing, China, analyzed scans from 250 university students and concluded that only child are more creative. This result was associated with a higher contact with parents (Short and Fengying, 1998). Similar result was found by Ohio University, which analyzed 24,599 students from US and concluded that fewer children can lead to a higher grade in elementary school.

Additionally it is less expensive to provide resources to just one kid. It is possible to observe that the Education system is getting expensive. In this way, the proportion of budget expended per person is also getting higher. It is well known that higher formal education increases the chance of getting better jobs, and getting more money. Data collected from University of Melbourne suggested that Australian men between the ages of 35 and 54 without siblings were in average 240 thousand dollars richer than those who had one sibling (Malacarne, 2017). When compared to other men with three or four siblings, the only child were 314 thousand dollars richer (Malacarne, 2017).

Hence it is not surprising that the number of couples with just one child is increasing during the last centuries. For example, according to the US Census Bureau, approximately 20% of the infant population doesn’t have siblings, this value double those 50 years ago (Doutíssima, 2015). The biggest reasons that suggest this change is that the Educational system is getting expensive and also the proportion of budget expended per person is also getting bigger.
