<Neuroscience as the ideal method for experiments in applied social sciences>

Neuroscience is a set of methodological techniques and knowledge that uses brain images and whereby is possible to investigate frequency, location and timing of neuronal activity (LEE, BRODERICK, CHAMBERLAIN, 2007), relating this information with a particular stimulus. In many contexts, it is still an expensive approach, although it could be a promising option for scientists who are concerned with causal relations in applied social sciences. Experiment is a research design derived from natural sciences that gives the ideal structure for inferring causality. This essay aims to give arguments for why research in social science whose methodology is an experiment must use neuroscience approach.
Firstly, it’s important to highlight that experiments are used to study causal relations (MALHOTRA, 2001), and its origin in natural sciences try to secure that its measurement is made by tangible results. The relation of causality is defined by Malhotra, 2001 as “ <when the occurrence of x rises the occurrence of y> ”. A relevant scientific issue is that in its point of view it’s impossible to say that something causes other, but it’s easier to measure this kind of effect when you have observable signals, such as that in brain images. Actually, experiments in social sciences are very criticized because traditional methods use people responses to investigate the results of the intervention, mainly because these responses can be biased by the intention of the volunteers. Researchers could minimize this problem securing that respondents don’t know the real interest of the investigation (MALHOTRA, 2001), but using neuroscience methods is a more efficient way to diminish bias impact.
Secondly, there are responses difficult to say or to measure by means of questionnaires, and neurosciences could reveal inaccessible factors through traditional methods. These can be a source of invalidity in experimental studies, and a focus of attacks by many scientists. The way in which people are in that specific moment or even the voluntary desire of giving some answer can interfere in the results. Researchers try to control these factors by randomization and statistical control, as suggested by Malhotra, 2001. To access not stated responses brain images can provide rich information.
Finaly, it’s an ideal moment for using neurosciences in research in human behavior. Like said before, it’s difficult to administrate the experiments, particularly in a field research (MALHOTRA, 2001). This complex field, that includes experiments in applied social sciences as marketing, needs a modern and trustworthy method to be investigated. The research addressed by traditional methods can be inefficient, since many factors involved in responses to stimuli are not clear. It follows therefore that the use of more modern methods is essential for social sciences. Neuroscience identifies how a set of choices can be influenced at the individual level, market or society (Breiter et al, 2014). These attributes allow the technique to be used to check the role of many factors involved in human behavior.
In this way, to use neurosciences approach is a strategic option when social applied researchers opted for an experimental design. It could facilitate because it deals with brain images, in other words, observable results, that is independent on personal will. Hidden answers could be automatically observed though the equipment, which increase the results validity. Globalization and instant communication between scientists in all field can make the development of this area faster because it is inherently multidisciplinary. Development in technology will accelerate the accuracy of these methods and diminish their costs, what also justify why the moment is perfect, and why neuroscience is considered a hot theme by many scientists.
