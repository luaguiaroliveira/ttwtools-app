<The importance of bioinformatics>

Bioinformatics is an interdisciplinary field of study involving Biology, Computer Science, Statistics, Mathematics, and Engineering. Because it is a comprehensive concept we can define a range of applications that relate to bioinformatics such as: analysis of biological data and molecular biology, development of algorithms, applied probability and statistics, genetics, sequencing genome and annotations, among others. Therefore, we will discuss important aspects in the application of bioinformatics in our daily lives, major milestones and notable researchers, as well as innovations and future perspectives.
Perceived as a new scientific discipline, bioinformatics has been developed especially to order the results generated from gene sequencing initiatives, their protein products, and the use of statistical methods capable of analyzing large amounts of biological data and also establishing structural relationships between genes and proteins. (ARAUJO et al., 2008 )
Another important point about the importance of bioinformatics is the understanding of historical facts related to the studies and discoveries of great researchers in the field. As an example is the publication of the work of James Watson and Francis Crick on the helix structure of the DNA molecule is an important fact to cite. The contributions of Margareth Dayhoff, an american physicist who devoted herself to the study of mathematical and computational methods, were also essential for advances in biology and medicine. She was responsible for creating the Atlas of Protein Sequence and Structure, an important database of aminoacid sequences and protein structures. In general, all contributions and scientific publications by researchers were decisive for the progress of bioinformatics.
Through the growing volume of genetic and biological information, the need for analysis and data sharing, the resolution of health problems, and so many others, there is a growing perspective regarding the expansion of bioinformatics. In recent years there has been a very strong role in genomic research coming from the Human Genome Project, which is the genetic mapping of the human genome in order to identify and sequence genes to correlate and support various research on disease, drug development, etc. <deleted citation>. (LUSCOMBE et al., 2001)
In conclusion, bioinformatics provides integration between different areas and solves biological problems through the evolution of scientific contributions throughout history. In the future, we may evolve into thorough studies of molecular biology and preventive medicine.
