Air pollution can be defined as the release into the atmosphere of any substance (particles or gases) that alters the natural air constitution, adversely affecting plant and animal species or causing physical and chemical changes in minerals (GODISH, 1997).

The National Standards for Air Quality, established by the Brazilian Institute of Environment and Renewable Natural Resources (IBAMA) and approved by the National Environmental Council (CONAMA), legally define the upper limit for the concentration of a pollutant in atmosphere to protect the health of living beings and the environment. To determine the concentration of a pollutant in the atmosphere, measuring the degree of exposure of the receptors (human beings, other animals, plants or materials) as the final result of the release process of this pollutant into the atmosphere from sources of emission, and their interactions in the atmosphere, the pontode physical view (dilution) and chemical (chemical reactions) (CETESB, 2011).

The passenger vehicles are recognized as a major source of air pollution in large Brazilian cities, making a recurring theme when it comes to control air pollutants. In Brazil, the transportation is based on roads, that is, much of the transport of goods is done via trucks, and transport people is basically done by bus or car.

There is currently a tendency to car users increase in Brazil, a situation that can be inferred by the problem of the great congestion. The addition of new vehicles, even some being more efficient models in relation to fuel consumption, increases the total volume of emissions, both for their own emissions and by increasing traffic congestion, leading the other cars to emit more per kilometer.
The main pollutants emitted directly by motor vehicles are carbon monoxide - CO, hydrocarbons - HC, nitrogen oxides - NOx, sulfur oxides - SOx, particulate matter - MP (total particles emsuspensão, smoke, inhalable particles and fine inhalable particles) and aldehydes (Szwarcfiter, 2004).

To think of a strategy for the abatement of air pollution in urban areas serve a good understanding of the nature and magnitude of the problem of pollution and the applicability of the various abatement measures is necessary, according ONURSAL et al. (1997). The choice of the type of measure depends on the costs and benefits as well as the monitoring and enforcement capacity of the responsible institutions. Normal measures to reduce vehicular pollution are those that mainly affect vehicles, fuel and transportation management (LOUREIRO, 2005).

Thus, given the great importance of good air quality for the health of animals and plants, the concern with the issue and control of atmospheric pollutants is necessary and appropriate for their continued increase. With more studies on this topic and with proper strategic planning and encouraging the use of appropriate technologies and alternative clean fuels, it is possible to make an effective control of air pollution generated by vehicles.
