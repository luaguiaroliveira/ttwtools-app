<EDUCATIONAL, FINANCIAL AND FOOD FACTORS THAT ARE RELATED TO CABEI DISEASE>

Dental caries is defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) as a localized pathological process, which begins after tooth eruption, causes a softening of the hard tissue of the tooth and progresses to the formation of a cavity. This biological process is closely linked to several external factors, such as health education, socioeconomic condition and diet. Each of these parts plays an important role in the development of the disease in question.
First, it is fundamental to understand that there is a direct relationship between the risk of caries to which an individual is subject and his or her condition of life. That is, a person with greater access to education on oral hygiene, periodic dental consultations, toothbrush and toothpaste, will develop fewer lesions of the disease. Therefore, we can say that caries is a polarized disease, which is more prevalent in low-income individuals. In addition, how the family group understands health and illness will directly influence people's hygiene habits. This means that parents who give more importance to the oral health of their children may decrease the chances of developing caries lesions in these children. With this we see the importance of health education being focused on the family group and not just on a person.
Another important point is in regards to dietary habits. Studies in the area point to a direct relationship between income and high consumption of carbohydrate and sugars. We can understand that low-income families tend to have more difficulty feeding themselves with varied fruits and vegetables, allowing space for more industrialized products to be consumed. In this way, the cariogenic challenge of the low-income person will be much greater than a person with good financial conditions who has a wider range of food possibilities.
In view of the above, we can say that a healthy mouth goes far beyond brushing teeth well, because there are several other factors that may predispose the individual to have the disease. In this way, it is essential to provide adequate living conditions so that the population can be empowered to take preventive care of their health, not just treating the disease that has already occurred.
