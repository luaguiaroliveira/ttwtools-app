<DISPARITIES IN FOOD CONSUMPTION BETWEEN SEGREGATED URBAN AREAS>

Low consumption of healthy foods (fruits and vegetables) and an excessive consumption of ultraprocessed foods (hamburgers, soft drinks and fast food) are strongly associated with unfavorable health outcomes such as obesity (LOUZADA et al., 2015). Residential economic segregation, degree to which poor and rich groups live separately from one another, results in an unequal distribution of healthy food (CASPI et al., 2012; MASSEY; DENTON, 1988). That is to say residential segregation can be a key factor in the disparities in food consumption.
First, coupled with the literary evidence, in segregate areas (poor neighborhoods) there are fewer opportunities to work and education, high percentage of violence and fewer parks or places to work out or buy a healthy food (DURAN et al., 2013). Provided that, they get less investments by government or private companies as well as a high percentage of risk factor for an unhealthy lifestyle and chronic diseases (KERSHAW; ALBRECHT, 2014). 
Another reason is the proximity of establishments which selling unhealthy food. Associations between segregated environments and the availability of unhealthy foods are beginning to be reported in the literature. Segregated neighborhoods are geographically associated with fast-food restaurants and 1.1 miles farther from establishments which selling healthy food (BOWER et al., 2014; MENEZES et al., 2017). To put it another way, non-segregated areas (wealthier neighborhoods) the accessibly and availability are better than segregated areas. Including price and quality of foods (DURAN et al., 2013; LOPES et al., 2017; MENEZES et al., 2017).
As a result of this systematic separation based on income, the access and the availability of establishments of food may be associated with inadequate consumption of healthy foods. If people who live in segregated areas, have greater access of unhealthy foods and less access to healthy foods. In conclusion, there is a greater probability of unhealthy food consumption in segregated areas comparison to non-segregated.
