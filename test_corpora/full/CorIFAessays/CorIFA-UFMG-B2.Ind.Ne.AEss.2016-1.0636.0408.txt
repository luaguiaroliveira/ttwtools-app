<Neuroscience as the ideal method for social marketing>
Neuroscience is a technological and methodological innovation consistent for the investigation of relevant research questions, as addressed by social marketing. Although still expensive and restricted for ethical reasons, the neuroscientific methods can reveal hidden factors present in responses to advertisements inaccessible through traditional marketing methods like survey, commonly used in consumer behavior research. This strategic understanding is fundamental especially for socially important implications related to the inductions of consumer behavior changes that improve life in society.
Social marketing is the current discipline committed to such changes. Its first approaches emerged in the 1960s. The emergence of the term is attributed to Kotler and Zaltman (Andreasen, 2008) and discipline aimed at marketing ideas to achieve behavioral change, which has become an important need to increase the positive effects of marketing in society, since they go beyond the company and customers and reach the society in its social and environmental spheres (GROHMANN, BATTSITELLA, LUTZ, BEURON, 2014).
From the perspective of behavior change as a goal of social marketing, a greater understanding of consumer behavior is important, which can provide a greater effectiveness of marketing activity. For Solomon (2002), consumer behavior covers "the study of the processes involved when individuals or groups select, buy, use or offer products, services, ideas or experiences to satisfy needs and wants" (Solomon 2002, p. 24). This essay argues that the "purchase" of ideas promoted by social marketing campaigns can be better promoted through the use of techniques of neuroscience, allowing us to enjoy the benefits of anti-smoking campaigns, against child sexual exploitation, against the direction under the influence of alcohol, etc.
Among the perspectives that can be adopted in consumer behavior research is the role of emotion, which are internal states of the person involved in their interaction with objects (Frijda, 2004). They are the result of complex interactions, and authors such as Bagozzi, Poinath and Nyer (1999) emphasize the need for further investigation of the phenomenon in the response associated with the consumption. This research addressed by traditional methods, can be inefficient, since many emotions involved in responses to stimuli are not aware of.
It follows therefore that the use of more modern methods is essential for social marketing. Neuroscience identifies how a set of choices can be influenced at the individual level, market or society (Breiter et al, 2014). These attributes allow the technique to be used to check the emotions involved in the assessment of social marketing advertisements, so that campaigns are more effectively designed and communicated.
