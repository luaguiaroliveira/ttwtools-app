Nowadays we frequently face news about how the global life expectancy is increasing, and life is getting longer. Our life span only recently has been so long, however, what drives the process of aging is still poorly understood. Intuitively, we know what it means to age, yet, that perspective changes from one to another. For younger people, it means growing up, while for the elderly, getting old. Even so, finding a strict scientific definition of aging is a challenge.
This contemporary phenomenon has numerous implications, and this essay hereafter will review a social aspect of it: the ageism, which is the prejudice or discrimination on the basis of a person’s age. The term is predominantly used in relation to the treatment of older people and was coined in 1969 by Robert Neil Butler. Our society tends to create stereotypes and discriminate people for the race, gender and physical appearance. We group people based on the characteristics they poses, and one of those is age. Ageism exists in four different levels: personal, interpersonal, institutional and cultural.
On a personal level, it takes part when one’s beliefs automatically sees and assume someone old would be fragile, ill, helpless or unfit for work. Those assumptions would be considered ageism on a personal level but, when those simple believes evolve to actions or language, for example, to speak loudly because one thinks elderly have bad hearing, it is considered ageism in an interpersonal level. As for the institutional level, we have the healthcare system as an example. Healthcare professionals often make assumptions about their older patients based on age, rather than on functional status. In the US, only ten percent of medical schools require work in geriatric medicine, leading to a lack of doctors trained for the needs of the elderly. Lastly, on a cultural level, countries view older people differently, for instance, in the Asian culture older people are seen as wise and experienced in life, are generally well respected and hold a positive status in their society. On the contrary, in the US, UK and France, for example, older people are dismissed as people without much value.
Ageism has significant effects on the elderly. Those stereotypes affect their self-esteem and behaviors, and may make them feel like non-contributing members of society. Older people should not be discriminated against, rather be shown respect.
