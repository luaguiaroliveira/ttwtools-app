Too much has been said about this statement, pro and anti, and for too long ever since. Yet, no consensus has been achieved. Some think that learn two or more languages since the earlier childhood may damage the child's brain with too much specific and, sometimes, confusing, information. Others think that the foreign languages apprenticenship should take place as soon as possible. Personally, I am with these last ones. 
As someone who currently speaks five foreign languages, three of them with a high degree of proficiency (French, English and Spanish), I can say that, except for the French, which I learnt when I was already a grown man, through the immersion natural process we go through when we live abroad, I was extremely aided by the fact that I started learn both English and Spanish very early back on my life. Actually, my first contact with Spanish has took place when I was eight, whilst I can track back my first contact with English since to 2002, when I was not older than eleven. Children's brains are more fresh and have much more synaptic connections to establish, which grant them with a sensible easeness to learn new languages besides their mother tongue. I can testify it with my own example. 
Moreover, no brain damages have fallen upon me due to my linguistically varied education. On the contrary, studies have proven - and I can speak in favour of them - that children and youngs who grow up knowing two or more languages are more skilled with interpersonal communication, logic, interpretation and social analysis, as well as high-level skilled on text reading and comprehension. I was always a good scholar in my Portuguese and Literature classes, growing as a trilingual since I was a little boy. In spite of any possible natural talent for languages, I do believe I got this skilled as a consequence of have been put in contact with foreign languages early in my life. 
In conclusion, I do agree with and support the idea of children being taught different tongues as soon as they show signs to be capable of learning something. There is no such thing as an "impossible-to-learn" foreign tongue - they use to say that about Finnish, Japanese, Chinese, Arabic, and so forth - if we are correctly motivated towards achieve the goal to be a polyglot. At the age of twenty-three, I was capable to almost fully master French, all by myself, aided by the fact to be in touch with French language twenty-fours hours a day, seven days a week. How much more will a child able to do, as long as if this child had the chance! I can personally guarantee that it works... and works very well!
