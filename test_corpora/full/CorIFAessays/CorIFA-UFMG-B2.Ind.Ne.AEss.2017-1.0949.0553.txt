The increasing of life expectancy is a worldwide phenomenon. There are many reasons why this has been happening all over human existence, but an especial attention should be given in those related to the last 80 years – that is because those years match with the most remarkable raise in the rate here approached. In this sense, we will stick to two factors that we consider to be particularly interesting: healthcare revolution and rural exodus.
Firstly, there is the development of a better Medicine related to the improvement of treatments, vaccines and medicines, which then could be used in more cases and with a more effective tax of success. In historical perspective, we can indicate the period of the Second World War as a starting point of this progress. Among the innovations that it has brought, there is the discovery of penicillin and the expansion of blood transfusion practice, both very important to decrease death rate.
Secondly, rural exodus has its importance attached to a better quality of life and access to healthcare system in the city. In this way, people were able to see doctors more often and, more than just treating diseases, they could as well prevent illness. In temporal terms, we can situate rural exodus as a phenomenon occurred back in the forties, during the government of Vargas, the man responsible to Brazilian Industrial Revolution, which attracted peasants from countryside to urban space.
Therefore, the importance of rural exodus and Medicine improvement are justified by their mainly importance in the process of prevention and treatment of diseases, precluding death and increasing life expectancy. This is a good consequence, since live longer is a primordial ambition of humans; but it comes to parallel effects unwanted by us, such as population swelling and reduction of economic resources available to all of us – problems that now we must seek to solve soon.
