<Autonomous Driving>

Many technologies have been developed to deliver both safety and traffic benefits. In face of that, the concept of self-driving, or autonomous, vehicles was introduced to provide intelligent transportation system and reduce the number of human-error related accidents. The data needed is provided by hundreds of specialized sensors and cameras embedded in the vehicle; all of the data collected is processed in real time by a central computer in fractions of a second to manipulate the steering, acceleration and braking. These vehicles can exchange messages and collaborate with each other and with the transport infrastructure. Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) should be legalized and become an element of road traffic.

Traffic congestion is becoming a huge problem for major cities. Autonomous vehicles affect traffic congestion positively and help to overcome the obstacles of time and space. By gathering and exchanging information via Vehicle-to-Vehicle, Infrastructure-to-Vehicle and Vehicle-to-Infrastructure networks about the context in which the vehicles are located, motorway signs warning traffic jams, accidents, and extreme climate events can be given for the driverless cars to avoid those critical areas, reducing traffic congestion and the number of accidents.

A recent report analysed the impact of autonomous cars on the incidence of traffic fatalities and concluded that driverless cars could reduce fatal accidents due to human errors by 90 percent. That number represents 300,000 lives saved in the United States per decade; it also represents a saving of US$190 billion a year in health-care costs associated with traffic accidents. “ <Today, car crashes have an enormous impact on the US economy. For every person killed in a motor-vehicle accident, 8 are hospitalized, and 100 are treated and released from emergency rooms. The overall annual cost of roadway crashes to the US economy was $212 billion in 2012. Taking that year as an example, advanced ADAS (advanced driver-assistance system) and AVs (autonomous vehicles) reducing accidents by up to 90 percent would have potentially saved about $190 billion.> ” the report, from US consulting firm, McKinsey & Company, emphasizes.

Driverless cars technology could also be an innovative solution to improve parking management and ease parking issues in major cities, with the report emphasizing that driverless cars technology could reduce the amount of parking needed “ <in the United States by more than 5.7 billion square meters> .” Driverless cars technology used to be seen as something fictive, however groups such as Tesla, Mercedes-Benz, BMW, Google, and Audi are among the companies with systems ready to launch.
