<New perspective about electrical current>

It is possible that the way electrical circuits are seen for more than 300 years is wrong. Since the 17th Century, scientists and engineers have been studying the current as a flow of electric particles through a piece of conductor. However, the growth of Quantum Physics in the 20th Century showed that the laws in the tiny world of elementary particles are not the same as the macroscopic world and one consequence of this fact is that particles can sometimes behave as waves, like water in the ocean. This work aims to look at the interpretation of electrical circuits and show how recent researches could change all the perspective about how the electrical current works.

In Classical Mechanics, objects have always a predicable movement. In other words, knowing its position and speed it is possible to determine the path the object will follow. However, in the microscopic world, the classical mechanics is replaced by the Quantum Mechanics and one of the most important differences between them is the Principle of Superposition that states that particles do not have well defined characteristics. As consequence small objects like electrons, the particle responsible for the electrical current, could exist in more than one place at the same time. Supported by this idea new experiments want to prove that under certain conditions electrons could behave as waves in electrical circuits and follow more than one path at the same time.

A famous experiment in quantum physics, called ‘Double Slit Experiment’ showed that electrons thrower against a wall with two slits behave as waves and pass through both slits at the same time if they are not observed , and behave as particles if they are observed, passing just in one path in the last case. In order to prove that electrons could behave the same way in conductors, a similar experiment was created, in which electrons flows in a low resistance conductor, passing through variable resistance paths that may be or not observed. As result of this test, we have the conclusion that when not observed, electrons probably behave as waves and pass through all the paths of a circuit but if this condition is changed they follow defined paths, like in classical electronics.

Using this different way of electricity flow, now it is possible to create many types of new circuits. One exemple of phenomenon that could happen is that electricity waves could flow trough the conductor without resistance, ending with the energy loss in electric networks.Conclusively, new experiments has shown that there is a big branch of studies in electricity waves. Although there are just few discoveries in the area, it seems promissing, in view of the phenomena and possibilities that the technology allows.
