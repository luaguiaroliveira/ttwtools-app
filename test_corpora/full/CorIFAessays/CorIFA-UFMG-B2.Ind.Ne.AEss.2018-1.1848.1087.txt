<The future of energy in Brazil and its challenges>

Nowadays, Brazilian energy comes mainly from fossil, hydroelectric and thermometric energy sources. However, these ways end up being expensive and are in danger of ending. An idea to create change and improve the quality of the Brazilian energy plan is to explore renewable sources of energy, using the potential of each regions. This essay will address these issues, focusing on Brazil’s energy history, why it should be changed and its challenges.
The analysis of the energy plan is fundamental to the orientation of the energy sector, which should guarantee the production and the appropriate use of the energy produced considering the amount of resources being used, to verify that if these resources are being used correctly. In Brazil, non-renewable sources represent approximately half of the energy sources, using Petroleum, its derivatives and Mineral coal, which are highly polluting sources, which could be changed for others alternatives. Brazil has great potential to use renewable energies, such as wind and solar, its position is favored in terms of winds, which are characterized by a presence twice bigger than the world average, and the territory is privileged in terms of solar radiation.
Antonio Dias Leite on his book “Energia do Brasil”, describes and analyzes the Brazilian saga in the construction of its energy sector. In this time, Brazil needed energy for economic growth, betting on large hydroelectric, uranium and fuel combustion. The energy plan remains the same since then, leaving no space for new opportunities and creating an energy monopoly. Thus, the aggregation of new energy sources will require adequate policies and resources, given the competitive gap between them and traditional sources of energy. An energy policy that does not enter into easy short-term solutions, as well as other Brazilian policies, which provide immediate political gains. 
It is necessary a policy that safeguards the energies and that encourages the appearance and improvement of new sources.
Therefore, Brazil could have one of the best energy plants in the industrialized world, however, due to domestic policies, it is difficult to achieve this goal. One solution to this is to raise the awareness of the population through lectures in schools and colleges so that they are aware that an energy policy that keeps the Brazilian energy plan clean, incorporating the new traditional renewable energy sources and leveraging the participation of other renewable energy is the step towards the future.
