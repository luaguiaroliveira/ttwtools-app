<Visiting a reserve of the collection>

In the framework of the International Museum Day, the “Secretaria de Cultura de La Nacion”, a government department of Argentina, has invited the people to discover pieces that they never saw before. The objects can be observed at “Museu Historico Nacional” in the capital, Buenos Aires.

The proposal of this different aspect of visiting is based on the possibility of knowing the heritage that houses the museum, which is not exposed in an orderly manner and is usually not available to the visiting public. As we know, most of museums have a huge quantity of objects in their reserve area, which is not available to the public when the expositions are in place. 

Based on this proposal, the museum enables the hoarding of the pieces, in good condition, with an updated documentation system that can locate the pieces and know their history, fulfilling the mission of transmitting the message that their collections sustain. Of course, this kind of experiment must be produced in short time, considering the amount of work to put too many objects available. 

This news caught my attention because the museum promotes a very different option of public visitation, considering the patterns normally used in exposures. That is, the visitors will have the opportunity to see pieces that was objects that were forgotten in a basement or in a technical reserve room, but that have the historical importance and conserves the patrimonial value to the Argentine society.

Certainly I agree that this visitation is completely different of a permanent exhibition and does not have the usual characterization of a planned exhibition work, since the rooms to be visited by the public were not prepared in the usual patterns. In fact, the number of items does not permit the same layout. But the main idea of the project is not illustration or design, but to permit anyone to observe a collection that was collected a long time ago, but never had seen by the population.

In view of the above, I consider the project as a positive and intelligent initiative of the Argentine museum. The acceptance by the public has been great and this study serves as an example for other museums, showing that innovative and low-cost solutions can help the museums of the country to increase the number of people knowing and visiting them.
