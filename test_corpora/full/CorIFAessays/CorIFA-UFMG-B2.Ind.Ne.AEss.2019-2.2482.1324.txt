<Scorpion toxins can save people in the future>

Peptides from yellow scorpion venom have protective effects in cardiovascular system in rats. Animal venoms are a mixture of many molecules, some of them are proteins or peptides (Verano-Braga, 2008). Peptides are smaller than proteins but also are composed by aminoacids. Cardiovascular diseases are cause of high mortality and morbidity rates around the world (WHO, 2008) although there are many treatments to these disorders nowadays. Find new drugs or techniques is essencial to save patients with cardiovascular diseases and improve their life conditions.  Two effects of this peptide were observed in studied animals: anti-hypertensive and heart protection.
These molecules or toxins from yellow scorpion were used in hypertensive rats and promoted antihypertensive action (Verano-Braga et al, 2010). When hypertension is not treated, some hazards can occur, for example, atherosclerosis and miocardial infarction, regularly death.  There are many antihypertensive drugs that show effectiveness and harmelessness, nevertheless the rate of convalescents that reach a satisfactory result in treatment is below of the expected (Guerrero-Garcia and Rubio Guerra, 2018). 
Other effect of these molecules was the reduction of heart rate and arrhythmias presents in ischemic hearts of rats (Ramos et al., Unpublished data). The frequency of ischemic heart disease is high in worldwide (Jaswal et al., 2011). Ischemia is absence of oxygen in an area because of artery obstruction. This event can occur in any area of the body, in this case, heart. This ischemia can lead to acute myocardial infarction, the heart muscle, and cause death in many cases (Bossar et al., 2018). When the patient receive medical care, one of the treatments is reperfuse the blood through the heart but this action also cause heart injury (Daniel et al., 2016); (Pesaro et al., 2004).
The aim of this research is study which are the molecular, morphological and electrophysiological cardioprotective effects of reperfusion post ischemia. To this, the peptide isolated from yellow scorpion venom will be used in ischemic hearts of rats. After register the heart functions informations, each heart will be divided in two parts to hystological and molecular analyses.
In conclusion, the hypothesis is this peptide will be capable to protect heart of injury caused by ischemia and arrhythmias provoked by reperfusion. With successful results, more studies will be necessary to know about possibility of use this peptide in the treatment of patients post acute myocardial infarction.
