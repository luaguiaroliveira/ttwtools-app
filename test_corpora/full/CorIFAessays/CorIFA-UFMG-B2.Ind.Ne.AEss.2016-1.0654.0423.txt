<Indiscriminate use of medicines in children with Attention Deficit Disorder with Hyperactivity (ADHD)>

It is a fact that the Attention Deficit Disorder with Hyperactivity (ADHD) is the most common neurologic disturb in childhood and it is marked by an ongoing pattern of inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity. Thus, in the last decades, it has been increasingly the amount of children diagnose with ADHD as well as the number of them who takes medicine to treat the disease.
However, some researches indicates that the use of metilphenidate – the mainly phychostimulant drug in treatment of ADHD - associated with the disorder in childhood has been indiscriminate due to changes in diagnostics criterias which leads to a extention of the population affected by the disease. Consequently, the number of potential users rises. Nevertheless it is not the only reason for increasing the consumption of drugs in ADHD. The other cause is the number of children who has been treated with overdose of medicine.
So, it is furthermore concern the real reason behind the rising consumption of drugs in children with ADHD. As soon, the use of metilphenidate has been so even misguided, being used as “drug of obedience” and as a performance improvement tool for children. In many countries, such as United States, the drug has been widely used to improve school performance and shape the children and, after all, it is easier to modify them to the environment. In fact, the drug should work as an adjunt to behavioral equilibration of the individual, together with other measures, such as educational, social and phycological. In this sense, it is recommended to provide public education for different segments of society without moral discourses or punitive attitudes, whose main purpose is to contribute to the development and demonstration of practical alternatives to drug use.
In addition, the consequence of this consumption in excess is dangerous due to the side effects of the drug associated with this indiscriminate use in childhood. So, addiction and abuse risks are some them. Thus, there is a potential risk of height growth impairment according to the Multimodal Treatment Study, the most important study of ADHD therapeutics established in United States. Some researches point out about cardiovascular risks associated with the use of methylphenidate.
Therefore, the association of methylphenidate`s use and the diagnostic of ADHD justify the increase observed in psychostimulant consumption. Concerning to the indiscriminate use, it is clear the link between excessive prescription and ADHD diagnostic criteria.
