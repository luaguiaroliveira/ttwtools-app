<The payment for blood donation in Brazil>

	The remunerated blood donation in Brazil was forbiden since the 80's. What have changed since the new Constitution of 1988? There are three diferent considered sources of blood around the world: remunerated, for replacement ou altruistic. What system does work better? Is it possible to use a hybrid system? Blood suplies are often insufficient for the regular consume and not rarely and elective surgeries most be canceled because there is no blood enough to ensure the procedure. Is it time to reconsider the system wich is working in Brazil. This essay will discuss the ethical and safety questions and try to propose changes in the actual system wich works in Brazil.

	Before the 80's, the paid out system of blood donation seems not be working poperly. Blood samples are very expensive and at the same time its quality were decreasing along the time. At this context, there were a revolution in Brazil, the paid out blood donation was forbiden and big enterprises closed these doors. The polititians in Brazil considered that altruistic blood donation are more ethical and secure. As a result, people woudn't lie about their health condition and wouldn't submit themselves for the donation just about his poverty situation.

	Countries like USA and Germany adopted the paid system to supply the blood needs. Other countries like Lithuania use a hybrid system to fullfill its blood banks. The safety of blood sample in this countries from the regular paid donners are considered better than the first time donners in cases of replacement. However, the ethical issues show us a conflict when we think about poverty conditions or financial difficulties.

	In Brazil, the high costs for blood donation campaigns that are not effective to keep supplies levels properly to urgents needs and bring us a discussion for what is wrong about actual system. Some rules like the prohibition to homossexuals who have stable relationship work in direction to make the problem worst. Maybe it is time to review the current system and flexibilize some rules and thoughts. An hybrid system wich brings benefits to regular donors, benefits like shopping discounts, tax-free and preferential services at markets or banks, free game tickets and so forth seems to be incentive to who has prejudice about blood donation.

	In this way, the system of blood donation in Brazil must be discussed urgently, because the actual model is consensually be not working. There are many risks about the paid out donation in cash and his unprepared implementation is obviously dangerous. Specialists must discuss exhaustively and propose a progressivily transition to a new and efficient model.
