<The importance of shared autonomy and its implications for teleoperation>

A lot of people say that machines are substituting humans in different economics activities hence, reducing job opportunities which were largely available in the past. But, is not it possible that instead of substituting each other they could work cooperatively? Aiming to study and understand the interaction between robots and humans in order to design and evaluate systems, a new field arose between late 1990 and 2000 from a variety of multidisciplinary fields, known as Human-Robot Interaction (HRI). In this context, both humans and robots should work together harmonically to accomplish a common goal.  Considering human factors, the interaction in teleoperation should receive an extra attention, since the user and the robot are not located the same site and the operator must have the ability to control it at a distance [4].

For this matter, [4] explains that there are many factors which have an influence in the interaction, being the level of autonomy one of  the most important due its performance increase result. During teleoperation for instance, it is interesting to avoid the user’s workload by the combination of automation and user’s control [3]. In this context, arises the term shared autonomy, defined by [1] as the tradeoff between a full automated system and full human control. This technique is important because it enables the inclusion of human in the loop while also compensating machine limitations in improve the system’s effectiveness to execute tasks.

The applications of shared autonomy reassure its importance. For example, the performance of a robot teleoperation might decrease in case the operator needs to be aware of a considerable amount of tasks, what results in burden and frustration [3]. Additionally, the communication delay between the user’s command and hardware execution also contributes for the performance reduction. In both scenarios, the teleoperation can be improved by increasing the level of autonomy [1]. According to [2], shared autonomy can also be applied in the industrial environments, enabling a flexible production system with lower costs and high-quality products. 

In the final analysis, it is possible to notice that study fields as HRI are mostly concerned about integrating humans and robots together to accomplish a common goal instead of substituting one by another. Moreover, it can be really helpful for robots to sharpen their environment understanding through human’s input. It is also beneficial to humans in an ergonomic aspect to avoid extra workload while also acquire better production levels by performing tasks quickly and reducing costs. However, it must be remembered that there is a tradeoff of the user’s control authority and a high level of autonomy that should be considered according to specific application with the aim to maximize perfomance.
