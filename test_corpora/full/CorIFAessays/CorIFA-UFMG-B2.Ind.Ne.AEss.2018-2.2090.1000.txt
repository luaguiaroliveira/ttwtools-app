Proteins are essential to survival of our body, it is required for pratically every essential function. They are a major structural component of hair, nails, skin, connective tissues and body organs. Proteins are complex molecules that consist of one or more chains of amino-acids, have distinct three-dimensional shape whose structural influence their specific function. Because of that  its important to understand the protein’s functions and how this functions are developed.

The study of the proteins origin is very useful to investigate the appearance of biological features through evolution. Rebuilding a protein allow us find the importance each part at all. The protein has some parts structure reponsible and domain, for example. On some drug study it is enables each part  of protein are the main active one to make tests. Learning about orign is a precise way to find  information about proteins’ structure.


Dating of a protein is commonly expressed by the lowest common ancestor (LCA) level of all organisms that possess that protein. This evolutionary study is made based on orthologys and homologs functions. However, a single protein may have different domains that may have been acquired in different time during evolution, implying that the protein structure has a mosaic ancestrality.

In this work, we developed a bioinformatic tool that allow us to investigate the differential ancestrality along a protein sequence. The software takes as input a protein sequence in FASTA format. To determine the ancestrality in different parts of the protein provided, firstly the protein is fragmented using a sliding window of length and step size defined by the user. Fragments are then submitted to sequence similarity search using blastp or phmmer against protein sequence database from Uniprot Reference Proteomes to retrieve putative homologs. The hits found for each fragment are filtered by an e-value threshold and the remained hits are mapped to the organisms which they belong to.

Finally, the LCA among the mapped organisms is determined by consulting their taxonomic lineage from NCBI Taxonomy. We applied this software using the following human proteins as queries: TLR4, CD209, ITGAV and NPC1. Analysis performed using blastp and phmmer yielded similar results and showed a large variation on the LCA level along the proteins. LCA level variation for TLR4 was 17 levels; CD209, 21 levels; ITGAV, 13 levels; and NPC1, 6 levels. The structural parts of the analyzed proteins were generally the oldest portion, which corroborate with previous studies on these proteins. In conclusion, the approach proposed by this work allowed a clear and easy way to investigate the mosaic origin of the protein structure and present potential application for studies involving the evolution of protein domains.
