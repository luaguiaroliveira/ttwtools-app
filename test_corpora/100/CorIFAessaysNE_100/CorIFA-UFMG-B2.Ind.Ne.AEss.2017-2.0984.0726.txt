<Detecting early the voltage instability to reduce the risk of electrical power system blackout.>

<Introduction>
Nowadays, the electrical systems are being explored at their maximum power transfer capacity and reaching their operational limits for economical and environmental reasons. The electrical system operates in a stable condition up to its limit, and when overloaded, it will enter in an instability zone. The voltage instability leading to the voltage collapse and/or blackout, resulting in large monetary losses, can be avoided if early detection and identification of weak areas are implemented. So, it is very important to develop some methodologies that could identify how far is the electrical system of the collapse point, determining proximity margins values. This distance, from the steady state to a collapse can be translated in indices,
relating electrical variables. The subject of this work is to identify the most suitable indices by simulating various scenarios, using data acquired from a real system (PMU – Phasor Measurement Unit) and testing standard systems available from IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronic Engineers). See one standard system in annex I. This last study is very useful because it is possible to compare the results obtained against others studies done by other researchers using the same electrical circuit.
<Definitions>
To understand better the situation, we introduce the definition of voltage done by IEEE:
“ <Voltage stability refers to the ability of a power system to maintain steady voltages at all buses in the system after being subjected to a disturbance from a given initial operating condition> ".
“ <Voltage instability is a status of the system when the voltage continues decreasing, in short or long duration, and the automatic control actions or manual actions, don’t avoid this drop> ”.
<Voltage Instability Process>
The voltage instability process can be a short duration event (ms to s), like a short circuit, or a long duration event (30s to 30min), like a on-load-tap-changer operation. In a heavily loaded electrical system where large amount of active and reactive power are transmitted over long transmission lines and in the absence of reactive power at the receiving end, a contingency like a line or generator outage, can lead to voltage collapse, resulted from a voltage instability condition in weak areas or non secure buses.
Beyond these factors others can contribute with the voltage instability:
- Load characteristic in function of voltage variations
- Automatic Voltage Regulator (AVR) of the generator reach its limit
- On-Load-Tap-Changer (OLTC) Transformers
- Incoordination between protection and control systems
<Methodologies>
To visualize the margin, curves P-V (Power x Voltage) have been largely used in the studies of operation and planning of the electrical system. The use of this kind of curve permits the comprehension of a lot different operation conditions with successive load increments. See annex I.
Monitoring the distance between the operational point (Po) to the critical point (Pc – point of maximum transfer of power) it is possible work in a stable part of the curve and to do an assessment of the electrical system operation. If the point Pc is overpassed the system will suffer a voltage collapse. The Electricity Agency (ANEEL) defines that margin must be greater than 4% (Stability Margin - SM) in active power transmission, to keep the system in a safety condition, with spare to supply power in case of some occurrence. To reduce this risk of voltage instability and blackout, a lot of indices were investigated to assess the electrical system to detect weaknesses and monitor the stability margins. These indices will be implemented in a SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition) or in a protection relays for an on-line monitoring.
At least 3 levels of actions can be taken:
- 1st : if the index indicates a risk an alarm should be issued;
- 2nd: if the index continues to decline and reach some level, should issued a command to block the on-load-tap-changer;
- 3rd: If the index is still lower and fall below certain limit, it should be initiated a load shedding.
<Conclusion>
Identifying the best indices and implemented via software or via a recipe to be followed by operators, the electrical system will operate in a more safety level, increasing the reliability and reducing the probability of blackouts. And energy means quality of life or even life when considered critical situations as a natural disaster, for example.
