<How long will the world food production system be a source of atmospheric carbon, whether it could be a sink?>

Since the industrial revolution the levels of atmospheric greenhouse effect gases (GEG), principally CO2, have been increasing, which is related to the rise in mean temperatures along the world (IPCC) (Crutzen 2003, FAPESP 2006). This temperature increase has already been promoting the rise of the ocean level, changes in winds and rains distribution, desertification and emergence of phenomena such hurricanes and flooding in places considered as secure until some years ago. Besides one the main source of CO2 to the atmosphere is the deforestation induced by the world crescent demand of food, the opening of new cropping areas goes on over time (Harris et al. 2013). However, the “good practices agriculture” has been defended as a sink of the atmospheric CO2, idea that does not seem to be well spread among farmers.
The balance between the CO2 absorption and release in natural systems, such forests, is positive from the point of view of atmospheric CO2 sinking, since most of the C absorbed by photosynthesis is changed to live biomass or as humic substances in soil (Cleveland et al. 2011). The land change to traditional agriculture, in its turn, diminishes the input of biomass to the soil and enhances the mineralization of the humic and other substances in the soil (Fearnside 2000). This process increases the microbial respiration, releasing large amounts of CO2 to the atmosphere, promoting the physical and chemical degradation of soil and inducing the need of new cropping areas opening (Harris et al. 2012). This apparently irreversible cycle, however, can be changed by “good practices agriculture” introduction.
Examples of good practices are: no till, release of the non cropped material to soil, diversification of cultures and introduction of species that release great amounts of organic matter to soil, being animals and trees also welcome. In these agrosilvopastoral called systems’ soil, the humidification process is more likely to happen (Agrisustentavel 2016). So, the CO2 absorbed by plants tends to be transformed to biomass and stable humic substances, being these systems sinks of this GEG. So, why are they not wide spread?
Cropping systems nowadays are under the control of big companies, which determines which transgenic seeds, pesticides and fertilizers are going to be used by farms. Besides this, the traditional ones think that agrosilvopastoral systems are not as productive as monocultures. However, a new generation is emerging and many of this young people consider environmental security is a serious problem that must be urgently solved.
The current scenario of the world food production system will be modified when this new mentality spreads to the country sites. It is hard to predict when it is going to happen, since young people are more likely to be in cities than in farms. But changes, as the creation of ecovillages around the world, are already happening, providing the hope of better future days.
