<Pain management in the dental practice>


A considerable number of orofacial diseases and conditions, like dental caries, fractures, root canal treatment, and wisdom tooth surgeries may be associated with pain. In this scenario, outpatients generally associate dental treatment as painful and, then procrastinate specialized service attendance what can lead to serious health complications. To reduce pain experience, dental practitioners should be aware of the pharmacology and the prescription indications of analgesics, anti-inflammatories, and opioids. This essay aims to present and discuss the rationale of dental prescription patterns of pain killers. 

Analgesic medications, as metamizole, acetaminophen, and acetylsalicylic acid, are among the most prescribed and consumed drugs in Brazil. The vast consumption of these drugs is explained by their efficacy, low adverse effects and also because they are available over the counter- this means a person does not need a receipt to buy formulas with the main active product. Dental prescriptions of analgesics are increasing over the years, which means, dentists are sensitive to their patients. However, despite all the benefits, analgesics work non selectively inhibiting cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes only in the supraspinal cord producing moderate pain relief. In this matter, dental procedures that may cause higher pain rates must demand the association or the prescription of other drugs, for instance, anti-inflammatories. 

Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are the golden standard to control dental pain. This is explained, first because the majority of invasive oral procedures induce an inflammatory response and second, NSAIDs have plenty of evidence about their security and efficacy. Despite the mentioned, ibuprofen, diclofenac, nimesulide among others, are low price drugs what play an important role in the society, especially in Brazil; a country full of inequalities in which the public health system cannot secure the whole population and the income of most of the families is a barrier to drug access1.  The success of NSAIDS also relies on the fact that one drug controls all the cardinal symptoms of inflammation- redness, swelling, heat, pain, and loss of function.  A research conducted in Australia from 2001 to 2012 demonstrated the overall increase in the dental prescriptions of these drugs by 32%, being ibuprofen corresponding to 68% of all prescriptions. Several clinical trials demonstrated that NSAIDs are the first basis treatment choice to manage dental pain, however, outpatients with low pain threshold must demand the disposal of opioids. 

Morphine, codeine, oxycodone, and fentanyl are kinds of opioids that act in the Central Nervous System (CNS) used to manage mild to severe pain. Opioids have been used for thousands of years in the treatment of pain; registers from ancient Egypt evidence that.  In Brazil, opioid use has increased in dental practice in the last few decades. However, the consumption rates of these drugs are considerable low comparing to developed countries like the United States of America. In America, the indiscriminate usage brought serious consequences as the increased prevalence of overdoses, dependence, addiction, and deaths related to opioids. Despite that, opioids play an important role in managing pain related to facial cancer, huge traumas, and trigeminal neuralgia, for instance.
