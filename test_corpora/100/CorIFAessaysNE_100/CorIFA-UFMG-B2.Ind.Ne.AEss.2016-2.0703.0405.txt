<Graphene: the strong and cheap 2D material>

If you have never heard about graphene before, try to get yourself updated, because this material has a big potential to change the world, bringing a revolution to technology. With just a stick tape and a pencil, the scientists Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov discovered graphene in 2004, which is a thin layer of graphite. Because of its dimensions, graphene is known as a two-dimensional (2D) material. Despite being thinner than a hair (it has approximate the thickness of one atom), this material is stronger than steel and even diamond.
Graphene has great properties that make it even more revolutionary. It is able to conduct more electricity than silicon, which is very useful in electrical circuits. It will not take longer and graphene will replace silicon in our technological devices, making them faster. Besides that, with graphene we will be able to develop a super thin, flexible and unbreakable touchscreen, so in a near future your cellphone will not be something fragile any more.
Moreover, Andre Geim believes that graphene could be used to increase the efficacy of proton-conducting membranes, which are constituents of fuel cell technology. It means that we could use insignificant amounts of hydrogen in the air to get energy to power a car, for example. Furthermore, the benefits to environment are even more impactful when thinking that graphene could make ocean water drinkable, what could solve the world’s water crisis. Some MIT researchers have discovered “that the water permeability of this material is several orders of magnitude higher than conventional reverse osmosis membranes, and that nanoporous graphene may have a valuable role to play for water purification”.
Samsung, the Korean electronics brand, has many graphene patents. However, institutions of research have been more efficient in the studies of this material. The University of Manchester, for example, has a great participation in research and findings related to graphene. They have at least eight group of studies related to graphene, with different applications and uses.
Graphene’s properties suggest that it can be a great candidate for the development of a fast and efficient bioelectric device like sensors. It could be used to monitor cholesterol and even DNA sequencing. It is even possible to use graphene in the treatment of infections. Currently, some researchers from the University of Bath are studying graphene to monitor glucose in diabetes, to detect cell oxidation both in a non-invasive way, what is relevant to diabetic care, and to reduce the risks of cancer. However, scientists still have to make a considerable number of experiments to make sure it is potentially safe to be used in humans.
As if that were not enough, graphene is still able to revolutionize global health as well. Some sexually transmitted diseases such as SIDA and HPV can become increasingly rare when condoms made by graphene get to market. By being an extremely strong and thin material, condoms would be indestructible and extremely slim. This fact would also contribute to the reduction of unexpected pregnancies.
Another area that has been highly studied is the energy storage. It is true that the electronic area has been increasing in a very fast way in the last years, however, it is still a problem for scientists to store the energy in batteries and capacitors. A capacitor can be charged very quickly, but it cannot hold that much energy as much as batteries. On the other hand, batteries take a long time to charge. Basically, developing a component like a supercapacitor a battery that could be able to storage energy without compromise is a potential solution for this problem. Because of this, graphene is being studied and used in experiments in order to act as a supercapacitor that can charge very quickly and be able to store a large amount of energy. The graphene supercapacitors can be used in electronic devices such as smartphones and if scientists succeed in their studies, these devices will be available for sale in five or ten years and costumers would not have to worry about the life time of the device’s battery, what would be a great improvement in the technological era.
Despite the various discoveries about the graphene and the great advances in research since its discovery, there is still much to learn about this material and studies will bring new applications for it in a near future. Nevertheless, no one can disagree that it can bring numerous benefits to the routine of people, and undoubtedly, we can say it is a futuristic and revolutionary material.
