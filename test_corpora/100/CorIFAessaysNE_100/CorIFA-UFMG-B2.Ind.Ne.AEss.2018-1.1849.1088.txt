<Women and Engineering>

According to historical records, the first institution destined to the formal education of engineering in the world, with a similar teaching model to what we know today, was Écola Nationale des Ponts et Chausseés in 1747, in France [1]. Despite this, the first woman to graduate in engineering was Elizabeth Bragg in 1876 at the University of California at Berkeley [2]. In Europe, the first woman to receive a bachelor's degree in engineering was the Irish Alice Perry in 1906 at Royal University Galway [2]. In other words, it took more than a hundred years since the creation of engineering courses for a woman to graduate as an engineer. Nowadays the reality has changed a lot, but women continue to be the minority in science and engineering. Although the number of women in these areas has grown continuously, they remain underrepresented at both the academic and the professional level. According to ""Conselho Federal de Engenharia e Agronomia"" (CONFEA) in Brazil, only about 16% of registered professionals are women [3]. Date from “Instituto Nacional de Estudos e Pesquisas Educacionais Anísio Teixeira” (Inep) also show a significant increase in the presence of women in the classroom. Between 2013 and 2015, there was a growth of 38% in the number of women enrolled in engineering courses. Even so, in 2015 women represented just 28% of the students [4]. Cultural values, social norms, work environment and styles appropriate to the male gender, and the relationship between colleagues of the opposite sex are determining factors for this scenario of female underrepresentation in engineering. 
Most women are not encouraged to be engineers. There is a stereotype that engineering is a course for men and not for women. It influences many women to believe that if they choose this profession, they will not be successful in their careers. This is a myth because women are indeed able to perform the same function as men and with the same productivity. There is no reason for women do not stand out in the industry. Year after year, we see young women equaling or surpassing men in math and science exams - both of which are key technical abilities for an engineer. The industry increasingly requires engineers who do not have only technical knowledge but also good communication and relationship skills to work effectively on a team.
In the history of humanity, the division of tasks between women and man has always been evident. From the earliest times, the woman is seen as a caregiver whose the main function is to care about the family while men are created to be providers and guarantee protection. These concepts rooted in society led to the creation of a stereotype of women. When we think about the children reality, since the first years of age girls are stimulated in their games to do household chores and take care of their children, while boys gain toys that sharpen creativity and improve problem-solving skills. Certainly, this tradition has corroborated to the idea that there are specific activities and roles for men and women in society. This culture at home must change. The cognitive abilities of children should be encouraged in a healthy manner in play regardless of gender. Sometimes women find resistance from their own parents when deciding to pursue an engineering career. In addition, the lack of female figures or mentors in the profession makes more difficult to inspire young girls to join the profession, a vicious circle.
The decision to enter this field still means for the woman to enter a male territory. Considering the historical line of engineering, this decision represents a rupture of values that discriminate against women [5]. Often in the academic and work environment, women are treated as less capable by colleagues of the opposite sex who disparage and undermine their work through judgments or improper jokes. In situations where a woman's position is leadership, many men do not accept being placed in an inferior position. As a consequence, in environments with a lot of men, several women tend to feel intimidated.
Finally, many women who are graduated in engineering have to abandon their careers because the environment is not thought to include them, especially after they choose to have children. This is one of the reasons brought by Leticia Ramos, a naval engineer, to explain the absence of women in her sector. She works at an offshore company, coordinating a team responsible for inject air and water into underwater pipelines. According to her, it is a promising area, with good remuneration. Leticia believed that the number of women working in the area would grow, but it stagnated. Many give up after having children because they need to spend weeks on board [6].
Engineering is an interesting and varied career for intelligent and sociable individuals - men or women. Few other sectors offer the opportunity to face serious global challenges, such as climate change, aging populations and, food shortages. Nor do they promise such strong employment opportunities or contribute to the economy on a large scale. The scenario has been changing over the years, but there are still many challenges to be faced. The worldwide tendency is that women to be increasingly recognized and represented in the various social spheres. However, it is important that industry, educators, and government continue their efforts and ensure gender equality. The market and, consequently, the country will only gain from a greater empowerment and appreciation of women in engineering.
