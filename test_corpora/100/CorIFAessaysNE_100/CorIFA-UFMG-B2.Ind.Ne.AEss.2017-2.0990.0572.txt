<Teeth Whitening>

Procedures that improve aesthetics are on the rise in Dentistry area, since more and more individuals seek a brighter and harmonious smile. One of many of those treatments and, by the way, the most popular in the world is the tooth bleaching. There are different types of the procedure such as: home bleaching, office bleaching or these two associated. This essay aims to demonstrate which of the kinds of treatments have more benefits, as longer durability of the result and more comfort for the patient.
Tooth discolorations can be of extrinsic or intrinsic origin. The consumption of certain foods, beverages, or tobacco products, as well as from inadequate oral hygiene or the use of certain oral hygiene products can lead to external discolorations. Furthermore, the darkening of teeth can occur when the surface of dental enamel becomes thinner, this is caused by aging or even by an incorrect brushing. An intrinsic discoloration is defined as one with its origin within the pulp chamber. This includes hemorrhage, necrosis, calcification, the use of certain medicine during the formation of the tooth and iatrogenic discoloration due to dental treatment.
Both types of discoloration can be treated, some cases only with bleaching and others requires the collocation of porcelain veneers. The treatment of external discoloration depends if the yellowing comes from a surface pigmentation, which can be removed with polishing by the dentist in a single session, or if it is a deeper discoloration, which requires a bleaching technique. Tooth whitening is a low-risk procedure, and therefore it is suitable for a wide range of patients. It is an approach that consists in the application of a product that contains active ingredients called hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide in the surface of the tooth, with the use of reaction potentiator or not.
In the case of whitening treatment, the dentist may choose among the different kinds of conducts. The office bleaching consists in a procedure using a bleaching product more concentrated and a little bit different in terms of composition. Also, the dentist can use a laser, which increases the reactivity of hydrogen peroxide or only the product and wait for the time passes. This kind of treatment will provide a faster result, but in the other hand the probability of sensitivity is higher and the tooth returns to the initial color faster. There are strong arguments, based on evidences, for preferring the home bleaching technique, using a less concentrated product, such as carbamide peroxide whitening gel 10% or even 16%. Since it is a less aggressive technique, the chances of sensitivity developed decreases and the result lasts more time compared to the other technique. It is important to remind that the result is exactly the same, but is more time-consuming obtained. In addition, by contrast with office bleaching, the return to the previous color occurs slower.
In conclusion, it is widely known by dentists that the safer technique is home bleaching, or at least this kind associated with office bleaching, due to the benefits provided. But a large part of them still opt for the faster and easy treatment. Moreover, even when the technique chosen is at home and the patient participate actively in the treatment, it is essential the correct indication, preparation and follow-up by the dental surgeon.
