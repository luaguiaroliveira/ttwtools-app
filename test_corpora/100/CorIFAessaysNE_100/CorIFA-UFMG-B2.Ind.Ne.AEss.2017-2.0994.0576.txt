      According to the Tufts Center for the Study of Drug Development it takes approximately 10 to 15 years to produce a new drug based on data collected from 1994 to 1998. Besides that the Medicines in Development for Skin Diseases 2011 Report, PhRMS (Pharmaceutical and Manufactures of America) affirmed that only 5 of 5000 compounds that enter the preclinical evaluation can be tested in humans. Based on this context this essay will explain the main points in the production of a new medicine, highlighting the reasons why it takes a long time to introduce a new product in the market.
Firstly, all kind of medication that is released to the common use have to pass through a series of researches and tests until it is approved by the competent organ of the country. For instance, Anvisa (Agência Nacional de Vigilância Sanitária) is the competent organ from Brazil. The regulation process of one medication is long, strict and it is really expensive for the pharmaceutical industry. Because of that every new compound have to be study before and after it is used by people to make sure that they aren’t injurious. This kind of research is called clinical trial.
	Before the clinical trial, have the preclinical stage which aims to verify if the compound is efficient and safe for consumption. Usually, this part of the process is performed first in vitro (without using animals) and then in vivo (using animals). This is one of the most important stage in the preclinical evaluation because it is possible to analyze the activity of a drug in a life form and select the components that are going to continue in this process to the clinical research.
	The clinical study involves 3 stages and it is a systematic process that follows scientific methods that can be applied to humans that volunteer to participate. In Phase I the drug is tested for the first time in healthy humans and the main goal is to determinate the best form to administrate the compound and what undesirable effect that it can cause. Only this stage can last 3 years. On Phase II the medication is tested in patients that have the disease related to the study. The purpose is establish a dose, obtain more information about the effects that it can cause and evaluate if it really works to treat the disease. This stage can also last 3 years. The last phase, Phase III, evaluate the efficacy again but this time comparing the new drug to the one that is already in the market and is used to treat the disease and it can last approximately 4 years.
	All things considered, it is easier to understand why it takes a long time to introduce a new product in the market. Besides that it is important to remember that this product is being created to treat a disease so scientist have to make sure is safe for consumption and that it works.
