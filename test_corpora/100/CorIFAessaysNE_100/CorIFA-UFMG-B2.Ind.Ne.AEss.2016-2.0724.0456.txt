<Which is more important: talent or hard work?>

It’s a recurrent topic discussed by people what makes an expert. Usually, who addresses this theme attaches to one of these factors: talent or hard work. People who defend talent argue that someone who is really good in an area is because he or she has a natural inclination to this area, and, independently of her or his experience, the person can be better than others. In contrast, there is who believes that because the effort lifelong people can improve their skills and become an expert. In the second idea, the abilities of someone are as developed as the time of training. Researches has established that skills are born of brain stimulation, indicating discoveries which support hard work defenders.
	Every knowledge becomes from external stimuli to us, either complex math skills or the hability to play an instrument. It means that our learning depends on our experiencies to improve and develop, even if anyone was considered an expert or an obtuse for an area. For example, the psychologist Tolman, creator of the study of Purposive Behavior, demonstred that mices that had contact more often with a maze, could, later, reach food faster. In other words, it was the development of mental maps that allowed mices find the way more efficiently. In this case, mices were not gifted of find food in a maze skills, they only learned how to solve the problem. It means that, even if some mice found the food faster than another one, if this other was more exposed to the maze later, it will be better in this.
	As another example of experiment which showed similar results, there is the Rosenzweig’s discovery, who built a really good place for mices, with various activities for them. Others mices were put in a box without stimuli like the other one. The psychologist realized, later, that the brain of mices in the first situation developed more and made a higher number of synapses. That is, more experience allowed experimental subjects increase their brain. Based on this experiment, if a external stimulus affects the brain, it sets up the brain, and this structural change allows a change in behavior. This means that who demonstrates interesting in an area, only will be an expert with training. Of course, this proposition does not disregard that some people have predisposition for anything; however, this is never enough.
	Therefore, in conclusion, people who say that experts are good because they were born with talent disregard that expert had to study and to train their abilities. Oftentimes, the constant exposure leads to demonstrate early skill, and, although someone may have a predisposition, this is, what we call talent, only the hard work might, in fact, progress the inteligence. Usually, who is considered a talented person is an interested that studied more than others, which can seems that this person is naturally skillful. Maybe, this interesting that is importante and moves people to learn and create. As Thomas Edison said, “a genius is 1% inspiration and 99% perspiration.”
