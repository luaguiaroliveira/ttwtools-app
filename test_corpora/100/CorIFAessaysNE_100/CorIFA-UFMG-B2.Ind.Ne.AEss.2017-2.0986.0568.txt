<Clinical pharmacist as a solution to reduce medication errors>

Intensification in the possibility of harm associated with medicines and prescribing can be defined as medication error (Dean et al., 2000). As reported by World Health Organization (WHO) in 2017, the cost associated with medication errors might be equal to US$ 42 billion. However, according to this Organization, errors are inevitable and the challenge is to reduce their frequency and impact. In this sense, clinical pharmacists can be an effective strategy, considering their expertise in medications associated with their sociobehavioral and clinical understanding.
First, reducing medication errors involves the capacity to predict, explain and elucidate problems related to medicines, which is possible to a clinical pharmacist. The pharmacy curricula provide more courses on drug chemistry than do those of other health care professions, which allows to pharmacists identify problems related to medications (David, 2009). Thus, dosage errors, drug incompatibilities, especially in those that frequently occur after mixing parenteral solutions, and incorrect monotherapy are problems that clinical pharmacists are able to solve based on their deep knowledge of medicines. The consequences to the health system are, therefore, a patient safety culture and fewer expenses related to medication errors.
Another fundamental fact that impacts the chain of a safety medication is the health education. Clinical pharmacists are able to instruct the patient about the best way to take, store and prepare his or her medications. In diabetes therapy, for instance, the patients who use insulin can frequently put the insulin in the freezer, which is known to degrade it, or do not administer this medicine correctly. The consequences of this can be a not effective therapy, which might increase the rate of hospitalized patients, higher dosages of insulin and irreversible harms related to diabetes not controlled, as kidney failure and blindness. Avoiding this means quality of life to the patient and a system of health care where the frequency of medication errors is minimal.
Finally, increasing a safe an effective use of medicines requires the ability to understand the sociobehavioral and the clinical science, which is provided in clinical pharmacy discipline. In order to identify problems related to medicines, it is necessary the capacity to interpret clinical exams, as high blood potassium meaning a possible inappropriate dosage of some medicine and, also, behaviors presented by the patient, as irritability, which can be caused by medicines that act in the brain. Thus, clinical pharmacists are able to optimize medication therapy based on their knowledge of the multiple abilities related to health care.
Therefore, an effective strategy to reduce the frequency and impact of medications errors is the use of clinical pharmacists. To achieve the aim launched for the WHO in the Third Global Patient Safety Challenge, which involves decreasing the medication errors by more than 50% in all countries over the next five years, a key method is to apply the abilities presented by clinical pharmacist, as deep knowledge of medications, health education capacity, sociobehavioral and clinical science understanding.
