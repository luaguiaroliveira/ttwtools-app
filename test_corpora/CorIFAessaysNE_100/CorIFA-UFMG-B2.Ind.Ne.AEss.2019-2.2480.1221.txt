<How can bioengineering move life to another level?>
Bioengineering or biomedical engineering is described as <deleted citation>. It is known that bioengineering has been around for centuries - maybe even thousands of years. As an example, a wooden toe prosthetic tied to the foot of a 3,000-year-old mummy from Thebes was found by archaeologists in 2000. Furthermore, medical equipment such as a stethoscope was first thought of in 1816 when a physician used a rolled-up newspaper to listen to a heart. By that way, this field has a broad influence, since the development of new biomedical devices until the artificial organs’ creation.
Developed by a group of scientists in Berlin, the Lorm glove gives voice to deaf-blind people, making them able to gain access to information in their way, and exploring the world never before seen. That device improves the communication over distances between a deaf-blind person and an intermediary, simply using a smartphone it can both send and receive message texts. It converts input on the palm into spoken and/or written messages, as well as incoming texts are directly conveyed to the hand via vibration signals. When a certain spot at the thumb vibrates, for instance, the letter ""a"" is being transmitted. The sensors for the individual letters correspond to the Lorm Alphabet and are located all over the palm. In doing so, the entire palm is connected.
Another important equipment is the 3d printer, invented in the 1980s, that apparatus can be used to create artificial organs and tissues, models of the human body’s parts and so on. In 2014 a 3d printed windpipe splint helped an 18-month-old baby, called Garrett Peterson, who was born with a rare breathing disorder that causes his trachea to collapse in response to the slightest movement, this 3d printed cartilage material was implanted to hold his trachea open until it becomes strong enough to function on its own. Besides that, Israeli scientists have taken artificially replicating human tissue one step further, by the first time, using a patient's tissue, it was engineered and printed an entire heart replete with cells, blood vessels, ventricles, and chambers. 
Nowadays, the bionic contact lens is more science than fiction. Created by researchers from the University of Washington in Seattle, a bionic eye mixed with an elastic contact lens and an imprinted electronic circuit allows the wearers to see the world by superimposing computerized pictures onto their natural view. Furthermore, that invention can enhance the way pilots drives, by sending information about routes, weather conditions or the vehicle itself. Moreover, it can be managed to monitor a person's biological conditions such as cholesterol level or the presence of viruses and bacteria. The collected data can then be sent wirelessly to a computer.
In conclusion, there are plenty of devices and technologies that can be used to enhance people’s lives, the focus was in three of them: The lorm glove to deaf-blind users and how it can boost their lives, the 3d printer in order to create artificial organs and tissues, as well as a bionic contact lens, which changes our perspective of the world, making us closer to the future. Whether the creation of technologies to replace an entire human body is considered science fiction or not, inevitably, bioengineering has been changing our lives in a way that we can not predict. The question is, what is the next step?
