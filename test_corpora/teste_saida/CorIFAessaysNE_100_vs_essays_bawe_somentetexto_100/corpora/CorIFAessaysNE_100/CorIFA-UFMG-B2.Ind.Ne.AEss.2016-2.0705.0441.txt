<Transgenic the dishes of others is poison>

Transgenic organisms, or genetic modified organisms (GM), are produced by transferring genes from one living being to another, often from different species. Transgenic plants are formed by extraction, isolation and cloning of bacterial DNA with the desired characteristics, and then inserted into the plant tissue. Currently there are some transgenic varieties of soybean, corn, cotton and rice released for cultivation in Brazil. The production and marketing of GM first announced as the solution to the extensive use of pesticides, creating more productive varieties, increase the nutritional value of food and reducing production costs. However, the production of GM seeds is far from being free of questioning. There have been reported cases of contamination of conventional crop fields, illegal planting and negative effects on agriculture in 56 countries. The release of commercial GM crops shouldnt be not given in Brazil without the extensive research about environmental, social-economic and health impacts.

Studies conducted over the past decades point several negative environmental impacts caused by planting GMs varieties. The increase in selection pressure caused by the indiscriminate cultivation of GMs favors the emergence of weeds and pests resistant to pesticides. Evidence suggests that the pollen of GM plants can be transferred to the domestic varieties and other species, causing horizontal transfer of genetic material changed. This can induce changes in the genome of native plants, threatening the genetic diversity. Moreover, the impact of planting GM crops on wildlife may last more than two years, mainly due to extensive use of herbicides.

The expansion of planted areas with GMs leads important sectors of agriculture to reliance on biotech companies that hold the patents of genetically modified seeds and pesticides. Some GMs are produced in order that their seeds are only viable after the application of a given chemical. In addition, several producers had losses with the use of transgenic varieties. In 2009, Mato Grosso producers obtain lower gains from transgenic soybeans than with traditional soybean; in South Africa, an area related to 80,000 football fields did not produce a single grain of corn!

The impacts on health, yet not vary studied, also exist. Reports of allergic reactions caused by ingestion of foods derived from GM crops already appear, and these allergies can be caused by the presence of genes that produce allergenic proteins. There is also the possibility of horizontal gene transfer to bacteria present in the human body and potentially increase in antibiotic resistance, as genetic engineering using genes which confer immunity to certain antibiotics as markers for identification of modified cells. The use of genetically modified products in food is still very new, research is limited and there are many doubts about the real consequences of transgenic intake.

Based on the evidence presented above and in other previously reported by the scientific community, it can be stated that control over GM and their side effects are ineffective and potentially harmful genes can be inserted into the food chain and the environment. It is extremely important that the political authorities society have a more critical look at the release and cultivation of GM varieties. Measures to ensure food security, social, economic and environmental should be taken on an urgent basis, such as creating stricter laws and the requirement for companies to conduct research with reliable and suitable results on the real impacts of GMs in environment to human health.
