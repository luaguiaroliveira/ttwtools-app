<Alterations in energy metabolism after liver transplantation>

Patients with liver disease often present changes in their nutritional status with the progress of the illness. Liver transplantation (LT) is the gold standard treatment for these patients, whom usually develop malnutrition before, and present increased risks for obesity and metabolic syndrome after the surgery, especially those transplanted due to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH). One of the possible reasons that are related to the excessive weight gain after LT is a reduction in resting energy expenditure, despite the different and controversy results in the literature about this hypothesis. In this essay, I will discuss a research that has demonstrated a reduction in resting and exercise energy metabolism in patients who had undergone liver transplantation as a treatment for NASH. Firstly, I will write about the decrease in resting energy expenditure. Secondly, the alterations in exercise energy metabolism will be pointed out. At the third paragraph, the differences between the genders will be explored. And then, I will make a conclusion about the results founded in the paper.
Singhvi et al. (2017) have found in their study that patients who underwent liver transplantation present lower values of resting energy expenditure (REE) when compared to controls diagnosed with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, with similar age, body mass index (BMI), body composition and markers of metabolic syndrome. That means that these patients expend fewer calories than the controls in rest, turning them more likely to gain weight even when caloric restricted diets are prescribed. This reduction in REE was also observed in another study with overweight patients 1-3 years after LTx for all the causes, not only NASH (Rodrigues et al., 2019). Regardless of these data, a few studies did not demonstrate reduced REE in liver recipients, such as one research developed with patients after approximately 6,5 years after LTx, with no evidence of hypometabolism (Ribeiro, Anastacio, Ferreira, Lima, & Correia, 2014). In fact, these patients often present an excessive weight gain after the transplantation, which is a crucial risk factor for cardiovascular diseases, the main cause of death in this population. Thus, it is necessary for dietitians to be aware of the fact that it may be easier for these patients to gain weight and harder to lost it. Understanding the causes of this tendency is crucial to make the best approach.  
The energy expenditure during exercise is also reduced in patients after liver transplantation due to NASH, which means that even if they make the same amount of exercise than individuals who do not have done liver transplantation, they will spend fewer calories. This reduction in the amount of energy spent during exercise can lead to impairment in the weight loss process in these patients and could be one factor evolved in the etiology of the increased weight gain. Nowadays, the research discussed in this essay is the only study available in the scientific literature that evaluates metabolic changes during exercise in liver recipients.
Authors have observed that all LTx patients evaluated present lower rates of energy expenditure when compared with controls, although it was only statistically significant in women. The mechanism behind this fact is not completely elucidated yet, and the authors claim that there is a gender-specific metabolic alteration after LTx, such as a reduction in estrogen levels, that is related to metabolic complications. Nevertheless, it is important to highlight that, women in the post-transplant group were approximately eight years older than women in the control group, which can affect their REE due to normal alterations in post-menopause phase. Therefore, nutritional treatment and caloric prescription should not only be population-specific, but gender-specific too.
The results demonstrated in these papers indicate that patients after liver transplantation, especially those transplanted for NASH may be hypometabolic and spend fewer calories during exercise, raising risks for development of overweight, obesity and making them more likely to suffer cardiovascular complications. Therefore, nutritional treatment is important before and after the transplantation, to explain possible alterations for patients and build specific strategies to manage them in each case. After the surgery, nutritional care should aim weight maintenance, working with patients in plans to raise energy expenditure, such as increasing protein intake and regular physical activity. It is crucial that more studies focus on investigating the causes evolved with excessive weight gain after LTx, to adjust the dietary recommendations and enhance nutritional care for this population.
