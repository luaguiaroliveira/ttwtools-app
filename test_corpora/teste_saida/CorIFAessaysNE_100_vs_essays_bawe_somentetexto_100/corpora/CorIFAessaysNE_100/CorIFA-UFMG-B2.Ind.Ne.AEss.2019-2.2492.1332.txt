The Brazilian society of today depends on discrimination, exclusion, and prejudice to exists. This prejudice is social, racial ou linguistic. In the book “Preconceito Linguistico: o que e, como se faz”, Marcos Bagno deals with the last type. Linguist prejudice is a

[…] silly and sneaky discrimination that the individual may have in relation to the language of the other:  it is a dislike, an ugliness, or misconstruction of a use (or language), without discussion about the contrary, of what could be beautiful or correct. It is a dislike without clear discursive action on the fact rejected (LEITE apud BESSA, 2015, p. 51, MARTINS, Rosemari Lorenz et al. translation). 

Using a simple and accessible language, he discusses what this linguistic prejudice is and what is the basis of it. This book is very important and valuable for Linguists since before its release this subject was not taken into consideration as today. In this essay, I will describe two arguments that Bagno gives about this prejudice, trying to show the reason why this Brazillian literary work is so important and revolutionary to this area.


The first reason for it is that the author explains the existence of an ancient confusion between spoken language and normative grammar. <deleted citation> (p. 69). Marcos Bagno discusses that a Portuguese teaching based on the grammar makes students insecure and afraid of the language because it does not improve their speaking, writing or reading at all. Just as a cake recipe is not the cake, the grammar is not the language. Grammar is the description of one part of the language, the written, literary, formal and ancient variation of it (the grammatical studies worked only with the written and literary language for more than two thousand years), so the alphabetic writing is an attempt of representing the speech graphically and conventionally. Besides that, Bagno demonstrates how artificial the written language is since all human beings learn how to speak before writing and reading. The grammar exists because of the spoken language, not the opposite. Also, the changes and variations occur in the spoken language, and this is the reason it is the one scientifically studied. This allows the conclusion that, in the opinion of the author, the duty of linguistic education today is to teach the students reading and writing in a way that they are able to be inserted in their literate culture. Only after that, they can meditate systematically about the language they speak, write and read.


A second Bagno’s argument that illustrates the importance of the book, is the explanation that the intention of his thoughts is not to leave the speakers imprisoned on their non-standard variations, but teach the formal language in a way they will truly understand and absorb it, and that means, considering their variations. Schools and education departments should plan their actions based on the diversity of languages (more than two hundred minority languages) and variations of Portuguese that exists in Brazil. And according to the author, educators also need to change some practices to achieve this purpose. Here I will talk about the one that I consider more significant. Bagno states that the notion of “error” should be reevaluated since contemplating the last paragraph, there are not Portuguese errors but orthography errors. From the scientific point of view, no one commits mistakes when speaking the mother tongue, as well as no one commits mistakes when walking or breathing. Speech is a natural and cognitive process and we only err on processes there are not natural, on processes that are learned, such as writing. Proof of this is that the following sentences could never appear in the speech of illiterate people, for example.
(1) *Aquela garoto me xingou
(2) *Eu nos vimos ontem na escola
(3) *Julia chegou semana que vem (p. 125)
These sentences are agrammatical, they do not follow the natural rules of the Portuguese language. Another example: students that are learning writing, could commit orthography mistakes such as changing J for G, S for Z and CH for X. If we have “chinelo”, “chiqueiro”, “chiclete”, why “chicara” would be wrong? So the student commits this orthography mistakes logically, doing analogies. Language is a logical process, grammar is not. On this basis, the author concludes that educators should try to replace the notion of error for the notion of an attempt to hit (and pedagogically apply it). After all, why educators show students that they are exclusively doing something wrong when they can use their errors to show how much they already know, and how logical their thoughts are?


Finally, the present arguments confirm how important the book “Preconceito Linguistico: o que e, como se faz” is to Linguistics, since it deals with relevant subjects such as the ancient confusion between spoken language and normative grammar, and how educators could rethink some of their teaching methods or attitudes. The main conclusion that can be drawn is that Macos Bagno brought to this area topics and questions that have always been here, but no one dared to give names to them. So, his action influenced, especially, the teaching of Portuguese and the scientific and linguistic view of the language. Unfortunately, we still have to deal with linguistic prejudice and its consequences, not only in Brazil. But thanks to this book and other following studies, we have been doing significant improvements.
