<The importance of testing materials for engineering projects>

In any engineering project, the choice of the desired properties in the material is one of the determining factors for the choice of the material itself and how it will be used. For examine the existing properties in materials, various tests are performed to compare results between them and identify the most effective way to work it, since they are subjected to many different mechanical stresses depending on the test done. These tests are extremely important to analyze the behavior of materials, such as metals, when subjected to various forces and different temperatures. However, for lack of knowledge on the behavior of materials, and often negligent manufacturers, many accidents can yet be caused.

When a test is not performed correctly, some defects cannot be seen and can cause accidents. An example widely used in engineering schools is the Liberty class naval cargo ship, built in the U.S. during World War II, when a large number of them sank during crossings in the Atlantic Ocean. The accidents were caused due to fractures in their structures that propagate quickly in the ship bulkhead, as during the studies of the metals used were not analyzed the behavior of metals when the ship would be crossing the ocean at low temperatures. The ductility of metals is proportional to the temperature, for when metals are at low temperatures tend to become weaker, fracturing with less difficulty. However, due to this design flaw, about one third of the Liberty ships sank.

Another example of lack of scientific knowledge in structures and materials were the collapse of the first Tacoma Narrows Bridge in 1940, in the United States. The cause was a design error that did not predict that the wind would swing the bridge in the same natural frequency until it collapsed. This effect is known as resonance. Whenever an accident occurs, an investigation is made to seek their causes with the intent to understand what factors or physical phenomena influenced so they can be prevented in the future. Thus, it was possible to develop new techniques and studies to bridge constructions, but are still registered a high number of accidents in this context.

The cost-benefit is an extremely important factor in any project, because for any company as there is less spending greater is their profits. By choosing to cut costs, various companies fail to do all the assessments and risk analysis necessary for the safety of the people involved in the project and the final product production, as in the example of American cargo ships that were built to a high demand and need low cost and speed of manufacture, resulting in catastrophic accidents during world war II. However, the lack of scientific knowledge is also a key factor in causing accidents like these, because for perhaps not having greater knowledge about materials, research and testing end up being more laborious and often infeasible in a full-scale production. Investment in scientific research makes it increasingly possible technological advances and greater understanding of the nature and how it works, so that we can apply the knowledge in all types of engineering projects, aiming to make it financially viable and provide all types of situations that they may be subjected.
