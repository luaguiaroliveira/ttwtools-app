<FUNCIONALIZATION OF POLY (TETRAFLUOROETHYLENE) IRRADIATED>

The poly (tetrafluoroethylene) - PTFE, known commercially as Teflon, is a polymer that has valuable properties such as high mechanical strength and high melting temperature. This fact makes the PTFE a target of many researches, including studies that propose the creation of new materials, from the combination of others that already exist. The biggest problem in manipulating this polymer is that it is chemically inert, which makes it very difficult to incorporate it in other materials. Therefore, there are studies about the possibility of modifying the structure of the polymer by irradiation with electron beams and gamma rays (TAEGER, a. et al., 2014). Thus, it is believed that it will be possible to mix PTFE with other substances, what leads to creating new materials that contain some advantageous properties of PTFE and acquire new properties of interest for various applications.
The poly (tetrafluoroethylene) was discovered accidentally in the United States, in 1938, in the laboratories of a chemistry called Du Pont. At first, this researcher did not realize the value of this polymer, but it was proved useful in World War II, for the storage of toxic substance, corrosive uranium hexafluoride, which is used in the development of the atomic bomb. In addition, it was useful for the coverage of the front of the missile cones at the War. Because of the success of these applications, in 1944, Du Pont patented PTFE as Teflon. Nowadays, it is recognized that Teflon has unique properties like: chemically inert - it does not react with most chemicals, which gives applications like waterproofing fabrics and wallpapers; high melting temperature - heat-resistant, which allows its use in coating of cooking utensils; high mechanical resistance to traction and compression - low superficial deformation when an external force is applied on it.
Despite all these utilities provided by the properties mentioned, there are negative points of these characteristics of PTFE, the main fact is the difficulty to react the polymer with other substances, making the production of new materials with Teflon very hard. It is difficult to incorporate the virgin PTFE powder evenly in other materials, by grinding and mixing, a conventional physical technique applied to other polymers. In addition, the chemical transformations are largely used in chemistry of polymers are not suitable due to the high chemical stability of PTFE (LUNKWITZ, k. et al., 2004). The mechanical pulverization of this stuff is difficult because of the stiffness of the polymer. Besides, the thermal decomposition is complicated, since it produces many types of toxic compounds of fluorine (KAGIYA, v. t. and HAGIWARA, 1975). Therefore, a different technique is required for modification or activation of the surface of that material.
Thus, modification of PTFE by irradiation with electron beams and gamma rays has been heavily researched, since it results in the formation of reactive groups on the surface of the polymer, allowing the combination of Teflon with other substances. Experiments done in the Laboratory of Chemistry of Materials of Nuclear Technology Development Center - Belo Horizonte, MG, show that, after the radiolysis (radiation decomposition) of PTFE in an atmosphere of oxygen, it is possible to incorporate substances at the Teflon, such as nitric acid, the Poly (vinyl acetate) chloride (PVA), dispersion of graphite and observe the structural changes and the several properties provided to these new materials.
