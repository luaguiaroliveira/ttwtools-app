<The irrationality of Decision Making>

Making decisions based on rational thought is the general assumptions of must part of researchers. In the opposite way, Ariely (2008), in the book ‘ <Predictably Irrational: the hidden forces that shape our decisions> ’, presents a series of scientific experiments, and findings that supports the existence of behaviors that may skew the traditional rational choice theory.
According to Ariely (2008) a type of asymmetric dominance or decoy effect is the "secret agent" in many decisions. Following observations, a number of situations influences the phenomenon of specific change in preference of choosing process.
In his book, Ariely challenges the theory of supply and demand, claiming that the methods of appointing a value can be manipulated. In some situations, consumers buy a product at a certain price, before they become "anchored" to that price moved by a arbitrary coherence. Thus, the consumers are susceptible to irrational pricing, making decisions without rationalizing the outcomes of their choices. In other words, based on memory rather than on preferences. In addition, Ariely explains the human reaction to the words "free" and "zero". Mentioning multiple experiments, the author shown that when person faced multiple choices, they commonly choose the free option.
Regarding social and market norms, Ariely speaks about the differences between this to elements. An example, of social norms could be friendly requests of payback and market norms as accounts for prices, cost benefits, repayment being essential, and others. Ariely talks about the influence of social norms into the market norms. He comments that the relations between this too elements could cause troubles. Such as people that prefer to do things when they feel good rather when they are been paid for that. The authors describe some situation in which work output is negatively affected by payment, even if will be a small or large amounts of money. Experiments made by others researchers cited in the book, confirm the assumptions of Ariely, This tests showed that “favor” works, in other words, done as a request of a friend, sometimes produced better results than work paid.
Probably, this effect on working live could be explained by an attempt to connect people on a social level, or a desire of it, in order to gain trust. In the author words " <money, as it turns out, is the most expensive way to motivate people. Social norms are not only cheaper, but often more effective as well.> ". Seeing in this perspective, the employer could use this effect in his favor.
In what it refers to emotion situation, the theory indicates that the human behavior is controlled by emotions. Ariely (2008) use data and experiments to argues that anger, frustration, and hunger named high-emotions potentially trigger effect s in the same level on decision-making. In this sense, the people are not what they think they are. It does not matter the experiences or live history in some subject, everyone could make irrational decisions all the time, because the behavior is under the influence of arousal whether biological, social, physical, sexual, etc. A positive aspect emerge in this context, researchers have appointed ideas to improve decision-making abilities, using emotion-provoking situations for making other life decisions. This is very relevant, because positive emotions could be use in live changes in irrational behaviors.
Otherwise, the irrationality of the decision making process seems to influence the lack of self-control on Americans. Ariely (2008) analyses two states of humor in which people make their judgments, he calls it cool state and hot state. If so, the theory also should be applied to other aspects in life such as health care and savings. Companies must be using this knowledge to manipulate the clients.
Specifically and individually, Ariely (2008) builds all the argumentation in the comprehensions of the irrationality of decision-making. Think about irrational decisions about ownership, each person could realize this theory, especially when they overvalue what they have. This idea makes them perceive the value of an object as much higher. Three reasons could be used to explain why thinking rationally about own possessions is difficult: 1) people worry more about what they may lose rather than on what we may gain; 2) the connection between the person and the things, makes difficult dispose of it; and 3) seen the transaction could influence the perception of it. Finally, the feeling of ownership it seems to be determined by the level of work the person spend on it, that is, the harder someone work on something, the more he/she start feeling about them as our own.
In the end of the book, Ariely (2008) states that previous knowledge about on topic could change the experience of it. Thus, expectations must shape stereotypes. The author concludes “ <expectations can influence nearly every aspect in one’s life.> ”.
