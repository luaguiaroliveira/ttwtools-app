<The social impact of fashion industry>

Fashion has been present in our lives for a while as a way to expression. Through clothing people socially comunicate. In the past, there were four seasons a year in the fashion business: spring, summer, fall and winter.  At the end of a season and the beginning of another one, new trends emerge and the clothes from the past season were left behind. Nowadays, however, it is observed that a huge change has arisen in this branch. The way to produce and consume fashion has drastically changed, making room for the “fast fashion”. Prices have become very low and we have a new trend every moment, reaching more than 52 seasons a year. These changes caused great social consequences that are becoming unsustainable and that will be discussed in this essay. 
According to the “The True Cost” documentary, 90% of clothes that were consumed by americans in the 60’s were produced in their own country. Today, this number has decreased to 3%, and the other 97% of the production are now outsourced in the developing world. The companies that compete in developed world, in order to adapt themselves to the “fast fashion”, making more clothes in less time and with lower prices, search for reducing their cost. To achieve the new way of production, the companies in the developing world do not have other choice besides making their cost lower reducing wages, ignoring security measures and using very bad infrastructure. In 2013, a building in Bangladesh called Rana Plaza, where worked a fabric factory, colapsed, leaving more than 1000 deaths and about 2400 injuries according to “The Guardian”. Also according to the newspaper, the workers of the factory had already saw and told to the managers about damages in the building infrastructure and the goverment had alreayd given an evacuation order, however, nothing was done about it. This event was responsible for bringing the reality of these factories to the occidental media and starting debates about this theme all around the world.
The global fashion industry profits three trillion dollars a year and employs one of each six people in the world in some sector of making clothes, according to “The True Cost” documentary. These datas make this industry the most dependent on human work, however, the return to the workers do not have this grandiosity. One worker of a fabric factory in Bangladesh gets about 3 dollars for a job Day, according to “The Guardian”. The competition of the companies for lower prices products is responsible for boosting the search for an each time cheaper labor. But what is the motivation to lower prices? The XXI century society, the most consuming of all times, have changed the clothes, that was use products, to consumer products, like the contemporary interpretation of Mark Miller, teacher at NYU, to the Earnest Elmo Calkins’s article in Printer’s Ink has shown. In this way, the clothes became each time more disposable, so there is a demand for lower prices due to the less time of using. With the low price, there is more sales and, consequently, more profit. 
Having said that, we can see that the excessive consumption is the main reason of this problem. To change this reality it is necessary that we, the consumers, became more conscientious and change our vision about the consumption of clothes in order to get this problematic reality better. In the other hand, existing more awareness about it and consequently having lower consumption, the economy of our capitalist system will suffer impacts. The capitalism, our society’s economic model, depends on this huge consumption to survive and changing in this would cause crisis. So, it becomes a difficult question that involves different values and visions. What we can do, in a micro scale, is to informe us about the origin of the clothes we buy and priorize the companies that show theirselves worried in garantees the dignity of their workers and have a strong positioning about this so, in cases of outsourcing, they do not deal with companies that do not follow this ideology. It is important that they really follow and fiscalize these things and do not use this only as marketing. An example of a company with this ideology is the People Tree, caractized for ensuring a fair, sustainable and with social responsibility trade. Another way that we can take is to priorize the local trade that we have knowledge about the fair production and with this, we not only help this cause but also boost the economy of our own community.
