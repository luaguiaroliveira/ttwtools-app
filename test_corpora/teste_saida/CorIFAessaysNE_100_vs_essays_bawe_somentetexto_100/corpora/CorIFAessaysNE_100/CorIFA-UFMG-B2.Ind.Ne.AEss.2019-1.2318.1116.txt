<Microscopic life: A look back time>

The relevance of microscopical life is easy to be underestimated or associated only with bad things like diseases and contamination. But, in fact, this kind of life forms is more common and important in our life than we can imagine. Every day we have contact with many microscopic bacteria, algae, animals, fungi and others without even noticing that. This essay will highlight the importance of several groups of these organisms on our daily life and for the history of life on earth.
One important group of microorganisms are the photosynthetic microorganisms, which not only are of essential importance to our environment in the present days, being responsible for the vast majority of the production of oxygen and fixation of carbon in the atmosphere but have participated on the earth atmosphere shaped and have a relevant role at origin of the life that is known today. In the beginning, earth was very different from now, with the primitive atmosphere being relatively poor in oxygen, being mostly composed of carbon dioxide and water vapor, a situation in which multicellular life as we know it would be very unlikely to form, given the central importance of processes like oxidative phosphorylation (which as the name suggests, depends on the availability of oxygen) to the high energy-demanding metabolism of these organisms. Luckily, after many years of evolution of primitive microorganisms, the anaerobic microscopical life had spread over the planet and some of them developed the process of photosynthesis, a metabolic process which uses carbon dioxide, water, nutrients and sunlight to produce energy, with one product of photosynthesis, being oxygen. The proliferation of such organisms allowed for extensive photosynthesis to eventually reshape earth’s atmosphere allowing for the appearance of more complex organisms which needed it to survive.
At the food industry the microorganisms have a strong importance too.
Vinegar, bread, dairy products are some of the examples that use
microorganisms fermentation to product. Fermentation is another way that the
organisms could get energy. This process produce gases and alcohol which
human learn how to use to have more variety of food. Although every living
being have their own microbiota. It means that microorganisms live inside their
body. And outside too. The stomach and intestine have many bacterias that
help the digestion. The cows, for example, can’t survive without their bacterias.
They can not really digest grass, the bacterias digest for them and then they
can use the fragmental protein. The termite only digests wood because they
have bacterias in their intestine. Human have bacterias which protect their
intestine against bacterias which could cause diseases. Is that why when
sometimes people drink probiotics. Those microorganisms colonize the
intestine, and occupied the space that other bacteria could stay. The body
recognize this kind of bacteria and notice that this is not a pathogen (causer of
disease) so they stay. Also medications uses microorganisms. Penicillin is the
first antibiotic and is a product of a fungi metabolism.
Another microorganism very common in the planet are the virus. Until
today have discussions about whether or not they are living beings. The biggest
justification is that they are not able to have their metabolism complete without a
host. However, some bacteria also can not and are considered alive. Viruses
are little studied, although this is changing, more and more its importance has
been noticed. Like bacteria, they are scattered throughout the world. And just
like bacteria there are pathogenic viruses and others do not. Viruses are able to
cut off the DNA of some organism and paste their own, this is how they multiply.
When humans understood how viruses caused diseases and how they became
immune to them, the vaccines were created. A vaccine is nothing more than
part of a virus or an inactivated virus, that is, structures incapable of transmitting
disease. Virus, probably, are present since the beginning of times and they
history still missing many parts.
Knowing the importance of microorganisms and their function in various
systems helps us deal with them in the best way. When they are simply treated
as something bad, disease-causing, an unjustifiable repulsion is created and
something that could improve many lives is discarded or has its implementation
delayed. Microorganisms are present in Earth's history since the beginning of
the first form of life. Understanding them is understanding the workings of life in
general.
