<Should the government invest in education?>


According to a research conducted at the Stanford University in 2015, an increase of 100 points in PISA’s evaluation (Programme for International Student Assessment) is associated with a two percentage points growth in Gross Domestic Product (GDP). This correlation is majorly associated with indirect economic gains long known by the economic theory. In fact, education can lead not only to a better society in terms of a moral speech, but also to economic development and lower levels of inequality. However, if the purpose is to reach a whole nation’s population, it is important to talk about the importance of public education within a system capable of not letting anyone behind. Therefore, if a country wants to pursue an egalitarian path towards economic development, its government should invest in education. 
First, it is necessary to restate the moral importance of education in terms of individual personal growth and social equality. In 1980, the Nobel Price-winning economist and philosopher Amartya Sen built a research, the so called Capabilities Approach, to determine what equality is composed of. In its perspective, ethics and justice depend directly of individual opportunities and poverty is understood as a curtailment of the fundamental freedom of the individuals. In this sense, public education can increase social capabilities and decrease general poverty through the spread of critical thinking and knowledge, if the government is capable of covering the whole nation population. 
In second place, investing in education will have the same impact in economy as any other kind of investment, with the benefit of increasing the quality of human capital in the labor market. According to the theoretic contributions of economist John Maynard Keynes, the results of governmental investment will be multiplied to the whole economy by the so called Spending Multiplier. In this perspective, public expenditure will impact the national income through the increase in general demand, stimulating the market prospects and the associated production revenues. Nonetheless, despite of this natural benefit, investing in education will also increase human capital and add value to the labor market as a result of a higher level of knowledge within worker’s class. 
Finally, this higher level of knowledge means a higher level of labor productivity, an essential prerequisite to economic development. In fact, from the standpoint of the Complexity Theory, richer countries are associated with a productive structure majorly based in manufactured products with a high level of value added. This kind of products require high quality and intense technique, which is not perceive in primary goods based economies. As stated in Eduarda Pereira (2017), <deleted citation>. Therefore, investing in education can add more technic and value to the productive structure of a country, leading to economic development. 
Therefore, it is necessary to state the importance associated with government’s investment in public education. As long known in economic theory, education leads not only to social gains, but also to economic development and inequality reduction, although it needs to reach the population as a whole. When the government makes this kind of investment, it improves individual capabilities, undermining inequalities and opening opportunities for an increase in human capital. It also activates the Spending Multiplier, which instigate the demand and, consequently, the national income. Finally, it ameliorates the productivity of the productive structure and leads to economic development. Those are the reasons why the government should invest in public education.
