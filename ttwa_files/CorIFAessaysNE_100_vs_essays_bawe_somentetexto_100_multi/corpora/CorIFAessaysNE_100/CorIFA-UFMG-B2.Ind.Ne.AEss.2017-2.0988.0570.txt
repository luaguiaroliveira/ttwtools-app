The traditional communities land demarcation has always been an polemic theme, specially in Brazil, the nation where the traditional people are approximately five million persons, according to the United Nations Development Program. The hydroelectric instalation and the big farming business are the most common complications in the recognition of traditional lands, like in the case of the Yudjá people, located in two indigenous territorries: the Paquiçamba Indigenous Land and the Xingu Indigenous Park. This essay will discourse about how these populations are afected by the Belo Monte hydroelectric for three reasons: failure by the company in complying with the operator plan, loud noises and explosions bordering the Yudja land and the change of the river flow and its ecosystem.
	The main cause of these conflicts is the refusal of indigenous communities to mvoe from their lands, and this occurs in reason of the indigenous attatchmento to the land, in case the land is the priority because of their cosmologies, in according to Philippe Descola in Les Lances Du Crepuscule or The Spears of Twilight (2006, p.6): “ <Whatever their specific purpose, all the anent (songs) of cultivation refer, in one way or another, to Nunkui. She is the creator and owner of cultivated plants [...]> ”. Besides the Yudjá conider their soil as sacred, some obstacles arose for the excution of the hydroelectric, because it is a mandatory requiremente that, for a work to be built, it must be approved and receive the operating license, and the construction company responsable for Belo Monte had not complied with the duty of receive financerally the affected indigenous acording to the Socio-Environmental Institute: “ <The decision, according to the news published on the website of the Federal Public Ministry, is due to the noncomplience of with the condition that determined the re-structuring of the local FUNAI (National Fundation of Indigenous) to serve the indigenous people affected by the work> ” (2016).
	Another factor that bothers the Yudjá people is the hungry caused by the explosions close to their village, which kills the fishes and scare the animals in the surroundings according to the Socio-Environmental Institute:
According to the Atlas of Fishing, important fishing áreas were extinguished or compromised with the beggining of the construction of Belo Monte, due to impacts such as artificial lighting or construction sites, use of explosives, launching or cofferdams (provisional river bus), silting of watercouses and grounding of river areas. With the definitive riverblock in November 2015, new impacts have been forming and tend to worsen due to decrease in river flow and conflicts in fishing areas, since many fisherman who lost their areas in Altamira and other regions are advancing to fish in the Indigenous Land, generating pressure in the territory. (ISA, 2016)
	Besides all the social side of the problem, there is an ecologycal side, that consists in the environmental problems caused by the diversion of the Xingu river flow, for example the displacement of species from their natural habitats and the delayy of their reprodutive cycles , especially in relation to fishes that depends of the water to spawn, as it is possible to notice in the Environmental Impact Report:
The Xingu River is becoming different along the way, within the Indirect Influence Area. From its encounter with the Amazon River to the village of Belo Monte, the Xingu River is influenced by the tides, forming islands and sand banks. These environments are important for the reproduction of Amazonian turtles. The Xingu river pedals - important environments for fish such as ornamental acaris - are located between the village of Belo Monte and the Paquiçamba and Arara Indigenous Lands of the Volta Grande do Xingu. The rapids and channels make navigation more difficult in this part of the river. From the river Bacajá until near the encounter with the river Iriri, the Xingu runs more slowly, with the formation of several islands. In this section of the river, near the Iriri, the pedals reappear. In the Belo Monte AHE Indirect Influence Area, the rainy season is different from that in much of the country. The rains occur more in the months of January to April. In the dry period, the amount of water that flows in the river is much smaller than in the flood (on average, it is less than 5 percent of the amount of water that normally flows through the Xingu River in the rainy season)(EIA RIMA, 2009).
	In conclusion, it’s possible to reason that the Belo Monte hydroelectric usin is affecting directly the Yudjá people in their traditional eating and lifestyle customs and the environment in its balance, and that the reallocation is not an option because of breaking with the patrimonial and ethical aspects related to the indigenous group. Unfortunately, due to the politic and economic interests on Belo Monte, in the future this question will still without an acceptable answer, and a provision will be taken when the water drought affects the high social levels.
