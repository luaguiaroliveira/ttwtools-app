<The importance of mammal’s surveys in urban parks>

Mammals are an ecologically important group of vertebrates, acting as important herbivores and predators of animals, besides playing a fundamental role in the dispersion of seeds and consequent regeneration of the forests. In this way, they play a fundamental role in maintaining the equilibrium of ecosystems (ABREU, 2009). In highly urbanized areas, an important aspect is the structural simplification of vegetation, which harms many species of small mammals and other animals, because resources such as food and shelter are reduced in quantity and quality (EVELYN et al., 2004). According to Chiarello (2000), in urban environments, small fragments support only small populations, being more susceptible to local extinction. These remaining fragments are of extreme importance for the maintenance of the local biodiversity and can also hold important samples of the fauna (SANTOS, 2008). In this context, the surveys of mammals acquires particular importance, due to the degree of threat and ecological importance of the group (PARDINI et al., 2003), besides being considered important for the definition of conservation strategies of natural areas (VOGEL, 2010).
Urbanization has a great impact on wildlife, as changes of the natural habitat generated by this process are drastic and comprehensive (MCKINNEY, 2002). Certain aspects of urban development promote the loss of species diversity. One of these is the species-area effect, since the increase of impermeable surfaces in urban areas reduces and fragments the area available for animals and plants (MCKINNEY, 2006). In some cities, more than 80% of the central urban area is covered by pavements and buildings, leaving few available natural areas (BLAIR & LAUNER, 1997).
In urban environments, different species of mammals adapt differently to anthropogenic changes, and usually only a small part is able to establish populations in urbanized habitats (HARRIS & YALDEN, 2003). In fragmented landscapes the extinction of species and the alteration of the fauna community have been documented by researchers in Brazil and in the world. The remaining forest remnants reach a high degree of importance in conservation biology, as they are responsible for maintaining part of the regional biota. These remaining fragments are of extreme importance for the quality of life of the people and for the maintenance of local biodiversity, they can also keep important samples of the fauna that are often unknown to the local scientific community and the population in general (SANTOS , 2008).
The knowledge of the species occurring in a given area is essential for the establishment of management and conservation strategies. Studies on fauna inventories contribute to scientific and popular knowledge, because they enable the making of species lists and further reduce prejudice and inform the ecological importance of the animals, allied to initiatives of environmental education. These searches enable greater knowledge of the fauna of the city and, in addition, connects the population with nature, contributing with the effort to maintain and recover the biodiversity of the city.
Diagnose and recognize the fauna of an area is a slow process and always under construction. The results of these researches may provide support for further work that results in the diagnosis of the situation of the group in urban parks. These data can also be used in the formulation of conservation strategies of the fauna of parks and their surroundings, and may also be used in dissemination and environmental education.
