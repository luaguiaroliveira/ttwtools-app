<Photovoltaic power in a decentralized system is better than the actual large scale, centralized and fossil fuel based system>

Our World is facing serious environmental problems related to climate changes and higher global temperatures. The consequences are reported everyday: the polar ice caps are thawing; the natural disasters are more severe; some populations are suffering from more diseases like Cancer and respiratory syndromes; the water is being polluted by human waste. Photovoltaic (PV) power in a decentralized system can be a solution to the impacts caused by humans when generating energy from large, fossil fuel based and centralized power plants. For instance, PV power fuel is clean and renewable (sunlight), it can be produced close to the consumers, and it does not require the destruction of large areas for constructing a big power plant.

Most of the power systems are based on thermal plants, supplied by coal, gas and oil. Those fuels are environmentally harmful within all their value chain: starting from their extraction until the power plants emissions. Thus, it is necessary to look forward energy solutions that does not use fossil fuels. The PV energy is produced by the conversion of sunlight to electric power, which means it uses a renewable, clean and abundant fuel to produce an essential human’s resource that is electricity.

Moreover, PV power can be generated in a decentralized way, which means anyone can produce his own energy. People can buy PV panels and put them on their roofs, becoming prosumers and protecting themselves from market volatilities. Even though it is still expensive, PV panels are used to “bring light” to many isolated and/or poor areas, for example, remote communities in Amazonia and underdeveloped countries in Africa. In addition, the PV technology prevents the transmission losses in long distances when connected directly to the load.

Furthermore, PV systems do not require large areas to generate power, because they can be placed on existing human’s areas, as roofs and warehouses. Some scientists are even developing PV panels to be installed on hydroelectric lakes. These prevent the destruction of natural areas to the construction of dams or large thermal plants. In Germany, which is the first PV power producer, almost all the solar energy comes from rooftop panels.

Although these benefits, some scientists and politicians do not support PV power. They claim it is expensive, less efficient than conventional technologies and, more importantly, its generation is random, unpredictable and uncontrollable. These are true concerns, indeed, but there are much research related to energy storage, operational and control technologies, ancillary capabilities and others features to diminish PV related problems.

Having said these, PV power should be a priority of governments around the World, because it can generate energy using clean and inexhaustible fuel, preventing us from an environmental collapse, it can bring people self-sustainability, it can reduce transmission losses and it can prevent the destruction of new areas on behalf of progress.
