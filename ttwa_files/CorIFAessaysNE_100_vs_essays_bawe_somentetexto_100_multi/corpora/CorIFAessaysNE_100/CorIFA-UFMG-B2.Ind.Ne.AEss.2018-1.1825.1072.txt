<Benefits of using high energy/power hybrid storage system for a suitable energy future>

In the last decade major attention has been focused on developing energy harvesting technologies using new and clean sources. Unfortunately, these kind of sources, such as solar and wind, are intermittent and need to be used in conjugation with an energy storage system in order to provide uninterrupted electric energy. An example is the wind farm that has its highest production during the night, when the consumption is at its lowest need. This essay aims to look at the new possibility of using hybrid systems (lithium-ion hybrid supercapacitor) as energy storage devices in grid systems or electric/hybrid vehicles instead of lithium-ion batteries or supercapacitors. 
The storage mechanism is of great importance in an energy storage device. The supercapacitors are known as high power density system, that means that they can charge/discharge within minutes or seconds. Their function consists on a physical process, basically charge accumulation on the surface of the electrode material/electrolyte. Unfortunately, they cannot storage a lot of energy and, by so, have been used in an external combination with batteries. On the other hand, batteries can storage almost twenty times more energy than supercapacitors, but within a larger period of time, typically, hours. This happens because the charge/discharge process in a battery consists in a chemical reaction that occurs in a lower rate than physical processes. In this way, the combination of both processes may be the solution to the problem of developing energy storage system for grid and electric/hybrid vehicles [1, 2].
It is well known that no conventional energy storage system is able to work in a high charge/discharge rate and also storage considerable energy. In the last two decades a lot of academic and industry efforts have been made in order to enhance common devices, as well as developing new storage systems, such as the so-called, lithium-ion hybrid supercapacitor. This system is an internal combination of supercapacitors and Li-ion batteries and can have a higher power density than the batteries and a higher energy density than supercapacitors. In 2016 a paper was published in the most important magazine for the chemistry research area, the Nature Magazine. In this paper, Gogotsi et al presented information that shows that the hybridization of electrical energy storage devices is one of the most promising alternative to overcome current limitations and providing future research directions towards the next generation of energy storage systems [3]. 
The hybrid energy storage devices are seen as the future of portable electric energy. The combination of the high energy – from the batteries – and the fast charge/discharge process – of the supercapacitors – can make it possible to use only one device instead of the external combination of these two types of systems. In the future years we may hear more about these new technology as they become more popular, especially in grid systems and electric/hybrid vehicles.
