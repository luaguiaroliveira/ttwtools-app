<Macauba fruit as a potential biomass source to biorrefineries>

The economic and social development based on sustainability is essential to assure the environmental preservation and, consequently, to guarantee that the future generations could have access to water, food and energy. One important way to do this is replace the non-renewable sources, as fossil sources, by renewable sources, as the biomass. The set of production process that convert biomass into products with great industrial interest is called “biorrefinery”. A lot of researches have been made to develop technology and knowhow to make viable the biomass conversion process (BAUER et al., 2017; NITZSCHE; BUDZINSKI; GRÖNGRÖFT, 2016). One important factor to be studied and analyzed is the raw material, which should have high productivity and low cost, characteristics from Macauba palm. In this essay, will be discussed and defended the use of Macauba fruit to provide different biomass types to produce bio products of great industrial interest in biorefineries.
Macauba (Acrocomia aculeata) is a perennial palm, native from tropical regions and it is found at several American countries. It presents high adaptive capacity to different ecosystems and to soils and high resistance to dry periods, plagues and diseases, this way it is the major palm dispersed in Brazil. The Macauba palm can be cultivated in consorted plantations with farming and other types of crops. Besides that, it is an alternative to reforesting degraded areas, since it presents the capacity to improve the quality of soils and to decrease the occurrence of plagues and diseases (CARDOSO et al., 2017; CeSAR et al., 2015). These characteristics contribute to increase the economic and environmental sustainability related to its cultivation and utilization, fact that makes the Macauba palm being a potential source of biomass to biorrefineries. 
Another essential characteristic that a source of raw material of bio products should present is high productivity. It is estimated that the Macauba palm can produce around 40.000 kg of fruits per hectare per year, which is equivalent to 6000 kg/ha of oil (CeSAR et al., 2015). This value is almost 10 times bigger than the production of soy oil, which is one of the major crops cultivated in the world (ROSCOE, 2007). In addition, it has been reported that all parts of Macauba fruits could generate products of industrial interest, as shown in the scheme from Figure 1. For example, SILVA et al. (2016) evaluated the deoxygenation of Macauba pulp oil to produce drop-in biofuels as green diesel and aviation biokerosene. MOURA et al. (2018) produced activated carbon from endocarp of Macauba fruit and evaluated its use as adsorbent to remove emergent contaminants. Thus, as a result of the high productivity and integral utilization, the Macauba presents potential to be economic and environmental viable source of raw material to produce several products with commercial interest.

To summarize, the Macauba Palm presents characteristics that contribute to improve the economic and environmental sustainability of its crop and utilization, like its capacity of adaptation and of improve quality of degraded areas, high resistance and high productivity. In addition, the integral use of Macauba fruit is an indispensable characteristic for raw material to be used in a biorrefinery. For these reasons, it can be concluded that Macauba fruit is a great source of different types of biomass to be converted into bio products by biorrefine processes.
