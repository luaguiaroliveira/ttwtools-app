<Benefit or a Burden? What is the impact of migration on the economy?>

The question around migration has taken place in modern age society. Some people argue that immigrant floods across countries and borders are a burden, as it steals jobs from native people, threaten the local culture and has a bad impact on taxes. Others argue that, as it has been for decades, migration has helped on the economic growth of the countries, and so it is a good thing. The point presented on this essay is that the impact of migration on the economy is beneficial for the destination and the origin countries.
The cross borders movements of people have increased diversity of societies and represented economic growth throughout the globe as history shows. However, some people, especially in developed countries, has the tendency abominate those based on the erroneous thought that it would change drastically their culture or “steal” their jobs. This kind of understanding has increased the number of politicians in far-right parties, so as the number of chairs they have pursued on parliaments. As those number rises the treatment on immigrants and refugees is toughened and withal that public policies for migration movements are left behind. The rough treatment those migrants receive elevates the prejudice and hate crimes become common. The natives begin to blame foreigners for the increasing violence as they start arriving, using this as an argument to expel them.
Nevertheless, there are those who think differently. The October 2013 High-Level Dialogue on International Migration and Development discussed the contribution of migration to the global development and to poverty alleviation, making this the central part of the international development agenda. With all that being said, it is important to highlight the benefits of welcoming migrants and refugees to those countries. Accordingly to a paper published by the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development – OECD in 2014, to answer the question whether migration is a benefit or a burden it is necessary to analyze the impact of such movement in three different areas: the labor market, the public purse and economic growth. Over the past ten years, migrants who have arrived to the United States or in Europe accounted about 47% and 70% of the workforce respectively. Besides that, migrants contribute significantly on the flexibilization of labor-markets, filling crucial niches both in fast-growing and decline sectors of the economy. Thus, labor migrants have the most positive impact on the public purse, with employment being the biggest determinant of migrants’ net fiscal contribution. Migrants also boosts the working-age population – especially on those countries in which the birth rates are decreasing and the population getting older – by arriving with new skills and contributing with capital development and technological progress of receiving countries.
With these benefits in mind it is important that the leaders from receiving countries hold a broad debate on the designing of public policies for those who arrive their countries in areas the maximize the benefits of migration like health, education and employment. Those policies will vary from country to country, according to the necessities of each one, keeping in mind the inclusion of them in the workforce will benefit their on population and elevate global GDP.
