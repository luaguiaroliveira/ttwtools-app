Nowadays it is vital to our society to increase a product’s lifespan in order to be more sustainable. This fact can be achieved by using a machining process known as deep rolling, which is used in the final step of a product’s manufacturing process. The deep rolling process plastically deforms the material’s grains, affecting its mechanical properties, and as a result improves the material’s lifespan and surface quality. Another method, known as shot peening, could be used to achieve these goals. However, shot peening requires specific equipment and may damage the surface finish acquired by previous operations. With that in mind, using a process such as deep rolling that enables a product to work for a longer period and with a better surface finish is fundamental for economic and environmental aspects.

One of the most important features of a product is its lifespan, and critical systems used in our society must have a high reliability relating to their lifespan. An airplane wing, for instance, is among those systems. Using a manufacturing process such as deep rolling can increase a product’s lifetime and, therefore, brings some benefits to the process such as reduction of costs and higher reliability. The first benefit relates to the decrease of the costs of investments to replace the worn products. By enabling a product to last longer, the amount of manufactured product required by the market may decrease, which in turn leads to a reduction in the usage of resources to manufacture them. Also, by lessen the number of products in the market, less row material will have to be extracted, reducing the energy spent, and avoiding environmental damage. The second benefit is the increase of reliability. Ensuring a longer life to a material as well as a greater stability to parts as important as an airplane wing and a car wheel is without a doubt very desirable.  Therefore, a product’s lifespan is something that must be considered in order to have a less costly process and more secure system. 

Another reason to use deep rolling processes is associated to the improvement of the final product’s surface quality, which is key to an efficient manufacturing process. One example of a material that has its surface improved is the AISI 4140 steel. When using deep rolling, the tool pressures the outermost layer of the material, deforming the peaks and valleys left onto the surface, reducing its roughness. Therefore, deep rolling betters the surface finish of AISI 4140 steel avoiding the use of other specific steps, which are strictly required to the surface improvement. As a result, it reduces the usage of energy used in the production. Finally yet importantly, the improvement of surface quality by this process decreases the time to manufacture a product. Making it indeed crucial to enhance the company’s production capability. Hence, the improvement of surface quality of the AISI 4140 acquired by deep rolling process is without a doubt very important when it comes to reduction of energy and time.

In conclusion, since it has been revealed by the situations just described, when it comes to reduction of costs and energy, and provision of a more reliable system, deep rolling processes are an intelligent choice for any company. By using this method, companies can better manage the usage of natural resources such as minerals and water. In addition, since less manufactured product will be necessary, the amount of waste might as well reduce. Consequently, companies may be able to produce goods in a more intelligent way, avoiding environmental damage and operating with more efficiency.
