<ART AND MEDICINE by Bruno Athanasio>
Medicine and art used to walk side by side, but the French Revolution charged it, and after that, each one got separated ways. While arts were still in the more creative side of the brain (right side), medicine was side back to the left side, where things are colder and use to be more exact. Even though medicine could be developed when treated as a more exact subject, it lost the sense of humanization that the profession needs. In that scenario, patients became diagnostics and diseases, numbers. However, those two old friends should get back together to the benefit of medicine. Art can help medicine by developing visual skills, communication with patients and it’s a good way of extraction for medicine students and physicians. 
The first and most intuitive way that art can help medicine is by developing a better visual skill and observation, what can result in the student becoming an excellent medical doctor. In today's digital, image-based medicine, heaving a developed visual skill, like proportion, depth notion, and the 3D notion is important to analyze diagnosis and, when talking about observation it makes it easier to understand a patient's needs by just looking. The major example of how the visual skill can help make excellent physician is Frank H. Netter, born in 1906, New York, that started drawing human bodies and end up becoming a great surgeon, and, even more, important one of the best reference in human anatomy all over the word. Of course, is no needed to be Netter to become a great surgeon, but visual skill is important for this profession. As shown above, art can make a great medical doctor by developing not only observation but also visuals skills.
Secondly, art can create a critical consciousness that leads to better communication with patients, so students and medical doctors are not pushed back for asking important questions. But, how does it lead to that? The answer is in an experiment that took place in a class of Penn State College of Medicine where students partner up to paint a copy of paint without seen it, only asking questions for his pair to reproduce it. It showed how much easier is to obtain information with open questions, and when they stopped and listen. The same relates do patients, to listen more about what they have to say. Furthermore, when you have a critical consciousness, you are not afraid of asking questions, once you are certain of the importance of what to ask.
Finally, art can help medicine by promoting a joyful time, helping medical students and physicians relax. The number of ""burn-out"" rampant among medical students and physicians, what can be related to the heavy schedule, high responsibilities, dealing with death and venerable population daily. All that combined and, when it comes to a student, the youngness, make it hard go thought the day. What results in the rising “burn-outs” that creates a negative image of those people. Once the population sees that the ones that should be taking care of others live, they can not even take care of their own. In that scenario, art can be a way out of that heavy routine.
The two old friends should revive their relation. Even though medicine developed a lot since the separation (French Revolution), art can help a lot in the current scenario. The visual skill that arts provide is essential in the now digital, image-based medicine. The critical consciousness is a big help in developing better communication between student and teacher, even between medical doctors and patients. And in the scenario of rising “burn-outs” among medial students and physicians, art can be a way out of the heavy routine.
